## What was done

### ⚠️ Survival of the scripted players (Normal, one player, Open Warehouse, eight games each)
The number is the sales event each scripted player loses in, averaged over eight seeds. "Honest" builds a sound layout and stops spending; "keen" keeps buying and upgrades everything; "focus" pours upgrades into the sprinklers nearest the entrance.

| | honest | keen | focus | boss driven out (keen) | refunds after event 5, of 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2.10 | 10.3 | 15.1 | 14.9 | 10 of 19 visits | 5.6 |
| Slower boss only | 10.3 | 15.4 | 15.4 | 12 of 20 | 4.9 |
| Crowd change only | 8.5 | 10.3 | 10.5 | 2 of 12 | 9.8 |
| **0.2.11 (both)** | **8.3** | **10.8** | **10.4** | 6 of 14 | 9.8 |

- **The slower boss helps a little.** He is driven out slightly more often, and the two upgrading players last about a third of an event longer.
- **The extra early shoppers split the games in two.** On four seeds the players finish within an event or two of where they did. On the other four, all three lose in event 6 or 7.
- **Why the bad games go bad:** the extra dads in events 2 to 4 get through. On one seed that is 9 refunds after event 4 against 2 before. Each refund takes double cash since 0.2.09, so the player cannot afford to build (24 pieces by event 4 against 39) and never recovers. With a limit of 20, a few early leaks are a big share of it.
- **The scripted players never see the quieter end.** They lose by event 16 at the latest, and the crowd only drops below the old one from event 17.
- **On Easy the change is a wash.** Endless games, same eight seeds: honest 12.0 to 11.9, keen 17.9 to 18.0, focus 16.8 to 16.5. They get just past the crossover and no further.
- **How far to trust this:** the scripted players are poor at the early game and follow one fixed layout; a person adapts. But the weak spot they show (early leaks snowballing) is real, and it is the same one the halved crowd showed in 0.2.09.
