# No Receipt, No Mercy: how the code is organised

Read this before changing anything. It is the contract between the parts of the game.

## The game in one paragraph
Shoppers enter a store through its entrances (one per player, mostly on the left) and walk to the tills on the right (the Car Park has a second bank of tills in the bottom wall). Every shopper who reaches a till takes a refund, split evenly between the players; 20 refunds (5 more per extra player) and the store closes. Players build a factory (belts and machines that turn boxes into goods and sell them for cash) and security (towers that wear down shoppers' patience until they storm out). Belts and machines block shoppers, so **the factory is the maze**. There are 20 waves ("sales events") with a build pause between them. Each player has his own cash and pays for what he builds; income is split evenly. Every building can be upgraded along separate tracks (damage, speed, range, durability), every shopper kind has a tougher "super" version in later waves, and the store comes in several designs and sizes.

Dads are the special case the owner asked for. They arrive in a single-file "dad line", walk fast, ignore free samples and goods on belts, and **step over conveyor belts** (only machines, crates and towers stop them). Their patience drains every second they are in the store, so the way to beat a dad is to waste his time: solid walls, wet floor signs (he ignores the sign and may slip) and towers along his route. A dad who gets through leaves a smug five-star review.

## Layers

```
index.html, src/main.js      page, menu, wiring                      (UI)
src/ui/                      HUD, build bar, speech bubbles, review  (UI, plain DOM)
src/render/                  draws the warehouse on a canvas         (rendering)
src/audio/                   sound effects, music, DEB's voice       (all synthesized, no files)
src/tutorial/lessons.js      the training shift's lessons            (no DOM; a test plays them)
src/net/session.js           HostSession / ClientSession             (multiplayer)
src/net/transports.js        how two browsers exchange messages
src/content/                 every joke, and the director that picks them
src/sim/                     the rules; no DOM, no network, no clock
```

Dependencies only point downward. `src/sim` imports nothing from the other folders and must stay runnable in plain Node (the tests do exactly that).

## Simulation (`src/sim`)
- Import from `src/sim/index.js` only.
- The game state is one plain JSON-safe object made by `createGame()`. Its fields are documented in `src/sim/state.js`.
- `step(state)` advances one tick (30 ticks a second) and returns the events of that tick.
- `applyCommand(state, playerId, cmd)` is the only way players change the state. It validates everything.
- `canPlace(state, kind, x, y, playerId, facing)` returns `null` if that player may put a building there, otherwise a reason string (`'fit'` when a building bigger than one tile does not fit). The UI uses it to colour the placement preview. `canUpgrade(state, x, y, playerId, track)` does the same for one upgrade track.
- It is deterministic: random numbers come from `rand(state)`, never `Math.random()`. Keep it that way; clients rely on it to predict the game between host updates.
- All tuning numbers and all definitions of buildings, shoppers and items are in `src/sim/constants.js`.

Maps: `src/sim/maps.js` builds the floor plan from a design and a size (`createGame({ map: { design, size } })`; left out, it is the original 30 x 16 warehouse with one entrance, which the tutorial and most tests use). The plan lives in the state: `state.w`, `state.h`, `state.tiles` (one string; the characters are explained in `maps.js`), `state.doors` (the entrances in the order they open) and `state.open` (how many are open: one per player). Entrances that are still shut exist from the start and count as floor for path-finding, so the maze can never wall one in. Nothing may assume a fixed map size.

Furniture: the Food Court design fills the floor with tables, benches, planters, bins, food stalls and a fountain. Each is an ordinary `'#'` in `state.tiles`, so every rule treats it as solid; `state.decor` is a second string, one character per tile, that says what each solid tile looks like (`DECOR` in `maps.js` names the characters). It is `''` for the designs without furniture and missing in old saves. Only the drawing code reads it (and the director, to leave out jokes about a fountain or a burger van the map does not have).

The Car Park: its parked cars work the same way (`park()` in `maps.js`; the letters are in `DECOR` too). Rows of parking bays run up and down the strips between the marked floor, so the lanes between the rows are where production lines go. A row of cars is a wall, with ways through: one straight across every row at the height of the first entrance (the walkway to the right-hand tills) and others staggered from row to row. It is the only design with two banks of tills. `tillBanks(state)` in `path.js` lists the banks of any store (each run of `'T'` tiles along a wall); shoppers walk to the nearest till whichever bank it is in, so nothing else in the rules needed to know. `canPlace` refuses, with the reason `'bank'`, a building that would cut any bank off from the others (`banksOpen`), so a second bank cannot simply be walled up. The Car Park also opens its entrances in a different order: the two in the bottom wall last. Nothing may assume the tills are in the right-hand wall, or that there is one bank.

The Back Passage (key `corridors`, 0.3): long walls run across the store and cut it into corridors (four on the smallest size, five on the next two, six on the three-player size, up to fifteen on the biggest), leaving a hall three tiles wide along the left wall and the right one; the walls have few openings (in every other gap between two sections of marked floor, taking turns from wall to wall). `passagePlan(w, h)` in `maps.js` works the layout out from the width and height alone, and both the walls (`passages`) and the marked floor (`passageZones`) read it, so they always agree. Each section's four kinds of marked floor are in different corridors, one below the other, so every production line has to go through the walls' openings, and a whole line reaches from one outer corridor to the other: the crowd gets past it only round its end, through the same openings or the halls. Patches are two rows tall, one on the Corner Shop (two would leave room for only three corridors there; a store with only three, which no size has any more, puts the Press Shop and Gift Wrap together in the middle one). Its plot is taller than the size's rectangle and as wide (0.3.01): `passageSize(size)` takes the lowest height that gives the size at least its least number of corridors (`PASSAGE_LEAST`) and at least an Open Warehouse's floor, preferring one where the top and bottom corridors have a row to spare, so no Loading Bay loses a tile to the floor in front of an entrance (only the Outlet, 36 x 26, still loses one: the next height that avoids it has 30% more floor than the warehouse). The floor comes out 0 to 8% bigger than the warehouse's, 18% on the Outlet, where five corridors need that height. A game keeps the plan it was made with (`state.tiles`, `state.zones`, `w`, `h`), so a game saved on the older, lower plan resumes unchanged. The walls are ordinary `'#'` tiles (no decor); the renderer draws them as breeze block when the design is `corridors`.

The Strip Mall (key `horseshoe`, 0.3): the store is a U round a car park that is not part of the store. `lotPlan(w, h)` in `maps.js` cuts a block up from the middle of the bottom wall (about a third of the width, from 45% of the way down); the block's left, right and top edges are ordinary `'#'` store wall, and everything inside them, down to the bottom edge of the map, is `'#'` in `state.tiles` with `'O'` in `state.decor` (`DECOR.O`). So every rule (walking, building, path maps, the checks that keep the tills reachable) already treats it as solid, and only the drawing changes: the renderer draws `'O'` tiles as an outdoor car park (`drawLot` in `floor.js`: tarmac, bays and parked cars, a pavement with shop awnings and signs, a sign on a pole) and the walls round it as outside walls; the minimap draws it dark. The Strip Mall has its own entrances (`stripDoors`: all seven on the left arm, three in the left wall and two each in the top and bottom walls of the arm) and its tills low on the right arm (`stripTills`), so everyone walks round the bend; every entrance is 1.0 to 1.2 times as far from the tills as the first one. Its marked floor (`stripZones`) has sections placed to fit the U: centred in each arm over its whole height, and over the car park in the bend, as tall as the bend allows (thinner patches on the two smallest sizes). Its plot is bigger than the size's rectangle: `stripSize(size)` scales the rectangle until the floor inside the U is as near as it gets to an Open Warehouse's of that size (within 3% on every size).

Map sizes: `mapSize(design, size)` gives a store's width and height: the rectangle in `MAP_SIZES`, except the Back Passage's taller plot and the Strip Mall's bigger one. Code outside `maps.js` must read the made map's `state.w` and `state.h`, never `MAP_SIZES[size].w` (the menu's and lobby's size pickers label each size with `labelSizes(select, design)` in `src/ui/settings.js`). `makeZones(w, h, tiles, doors, design)` takes the design as a fifth argument: designs with a layout of their own (the Back Passage, the Strip Mall) lay their marked floor their own way (without it, the ordinary layout). `tools/line-check.mjs` finds the sections from the marked floor itself (each run of columns with any), so it works on both.

Marked floor: `state.zones` is a string like `state.tiles`, one character per tile: a key of `ZONES` (`u` Loading Bay, `p` Press Shop, `k` Gift Wrap, `c` Shipping) or a space. A building whose definition has a `zone` may only be placed on that kind of floor (`canPlace` returns `'zone'`). `makeZones()` in `maps.js` lays the patches out so that different kinds never touch, which is what makes belts necessary. An empty string means no marked floor: the bare default map used by most tests. The tutorial turns it on with `createGame({ training: true, zones: true })`.

Players who drop out: a player who goes offline keeps his money for `TUNE.graceSecs` (90 seconds of real time: `player.gone` counts how long he has been away in ticks at normal speed, so each tick at 3x speed counts for a third, at 5x a fifth). If he is not back by then, `step()` shares it out between the players still connected, and he returns with none. A host who leaves gets the same grace. In the lobby nothing changes hands.

Pausing (0.3.02): `state.pause` is `{ by }` (the id of the player who paused) while the game is paused, and absent otherwise (as in every save from before it, and in every game the scripted players play). The command is `{ c: 'pause', on }` (`on` true pauses, false resumes; left out, it flips): any connected player may send it, Head Office too, in the phases `'cards'`, `'build'` and `'wave'` (in the lobby it is refused with `'lobby'`, once the game is won or lost with `'over'`); pausing a paused game, or resuming one that is not, is a yes that changes nothing. When it changes, it sends the event `pause {on, by}`. While paused, `step()` returns at once, before even `state.tick` moves, exactly as in the lobby: no shoppers, belts, timers, build countdown, card deal or grace periods for players who left; so a game paused for any number of steps and then resumed carries on exactly as one never paused. `applyCommand` refuses everything with `'paused'` except `pause`, `speed`, `role` and `skipCards` (the deal waits all the same); a rebuild paid for before the pause waits through it and goes back once the game carries on; `canPlace` and `canUpgrade` give `'paused'` too, so the preview turns red. The reason nothing can be built: the build pause between sales events runs on a timer that stands still while paused, and building in a pause would make that timer, and the pressure of a sales event, meaningless. Players joining, leaving and coming back are not commands (`addPlayer`, `setPlayerOnline`) and work as ever; the state, pause included, goes to a joining player and into a save, so both land paused.

Money: `state.players[i].cash`. There is no shared pot. `earn()` and `lose()` in `state.js` split income and refunds evenly between the connected players; `teamCash(state)` adds everyone's up for display.

Goods sold (0.3.03): `state.sales` is the running total, in whole dollars before the split, of what goods reaching a Shipping Chute have been paid for over the whole game: `offer()` in `step.js` adds each sale's price, and nothing else adds to it (not the end-of-event bonus, the boss's bounty, a warranty-card purchase at a stand, the restocking fee, nor the takings `skipBuild()` pays in one go when the doors open early: it puts the total back as it was, since those sales stand for time that is never played). It is never reset during a game (unlike `ws.earned`); a new game starts at 0, and a save from before it lacks it, which reads as 0. No rule reads it and the checksum leaves it out (it moves only with a sale, which moves `cash`, which the checksum covers). It exists for the INCOME panel below.

Income per minute on screen (0.3.03, the owner's request): `src/ui/rate.js` (pure, tested in Node) keeps one sample of `[state.tick, state.sales]` a second of game time and reads the store's dollars a minute across the last 60 seconds of ticks (across what there is from 10 seconds on, a dash before), so the figure is the same at any game speed and stands still while paused; a clock or total that goes back (a new game, back to the lobby, a loaded save) drops the history instead of showing a spike. A trend mark compares the minute with the one before (5% and $10 a minute either way; none until there are two minutes). `src/ui/income.js` is the panel: the whole store's figure, and with two or more store players "split N ways: $X / min each", N being `shareCount(state)`, the players `earn()` pays right now (Head Office sees the same, worded for the store). Shown in the build phase and sales events only (not the lobby, the deal, training, or once the game is won or lost). On a big screen it is the third panel in the left column (ON SHIFT, HAND, INCOME), as wide as ON SHIFT, placed by `placeStrip` in `game.js` with `sidePlace`: under the strip (under ON SHIFT without cards) unless that covers an open entrance, a till, the boss bar, the small map or the chat button, then beside the panel above it by the strip's own rules; hovering or focusing it shows a tooltip with what is and is not counted. On a small screen it is a chip ("+$1,240/min") in the top-left row after the strip (`placeChip`; on a second row under the players button if the row is full), and tapping it opens a card with the rest that stays until it is tapped again, something else is tapped, or its × is pressed. It never resizes anything and a click on it never reaches the floor.

Upgrades: `src/sim/upgrades.js`. Each building has up to four upgrade tracks, bought separately: `dmg` (damage, or the building's main effect), `spd` (fires or works faster), `rng` (range) and `dur` (durability). Which tracks a kind has, what a level does and what it costs are in `BUILD[kind].up`. The levels bought are in `b.u`, for example `{ dmg: 2, dur: 1 }` (absent means none). `tracksOf(kind)` lists a kind's tracks, `levelIn(b, track)` and `maxIn(kind, track)` give where it is and how far it goes, `upgradeCost(b, track)` the price of the next level, and `levelOf(b)` the number drawn on the badge (1 plus all levels bought). `statsOf(b)` gives the building's numbers with its upgrades applied and is the only place the simulation or the interface should read damage, range, firing time, a machine's working time or the hits it survives from. The command is `{ c: 'upgrade', x, y, t }` (`t` is the track; left out, the kind's first track). `src/ui/upgradetext.js` turns a track into words ("Damage 2/8, hit 9.4 → 11.7") for the building sheet, the build bar and the handbook.

Buildings bigger than one tile: `BUILD[kind].size` is `[width, height]` when facing east or west; facing north or south swaps them. The building lives on its top-left tile (the anchor); every other tile it covers holds `{ k: sameKind, part: anchorIndex }`. Always go through `anchorOf(state, tileIndex)` to get from any covered tile to the building, `tilesOf(state, anchorIndex)` or `footprint(kind, x, y, facing, width)` for the tiles, and `centreOf(state, anchorIndex)` for where it shoots or reaches from. The Barcode Laser (key `railgun`: it was called the Rail Gun until 0.2.06, and the key was kept so that saved games and commands still work) is 3 x 1 and fires out of its front end along the way it faces; the TV Salesman is 2 x 2.

Wrecked buildings: a gym bro's hit adds one to `b.hit`; a building is wrecked when that reaches `statsOf(b).hits` (1 unless it has durability levels or the kind says more), and every `b.hit` is cleared when the wave ends. A wrecked building leaves an entry in `state.rubble` that remembers its kind, facing, owner, upgrades and what was spent on it. `rebuildDeal(entry, cash)` works out what its owner pays to put it back: half of what was spent, with all its upgrades; if he cannot afford that, half the building's price with as many of the upgrades as he can afford at half price (the rest are lost). The `rebuild` command (the B key, the Rebuild buttons) orders it for all of a player's rubble (below), and placing the same kind on its own rubble uses the same deal.

Rebuilding is asked for, not automatic (0.3.04; auto-rebuild, 0.2.15 to 0.3.03, is gone: the owner found it made the game too passive). The `rebuild` command (`{ c: 'rebuild' }`, `all: true` for everybody's) is allowed in the build phase and a sales event only, not while paused (`'paused'`), not during the deal (`'cards'`) and not for Head Office (`'office'`). `orderRebuilds()` in `commands.js` goes through the sender's wrecks oldest first, leaving out ones already ordered: rubble that has been built over is tidied away (which is why the session forwards a refused `rebuild` to its clients); a wreck he cannot afford even the bare building for (`rebuildDeal` gives null) is not ordered (the owner's decision: he presses again when he has the money); any other is ordered: `deal.pay` leaves his cash at once and the wreck gets `by` (his id) and `res` (the whole dollars held for it). A record without `by` is not ordered (test `by !== undefined`). It then goes back at once if it can. The result is `{ ok: true, n, short, held, paid }` (put back now, how many of those lost upgrades, ordered and waiting, money taken), or `{ ok: false, why }` with `'cash'` (too broke for any), `'ordered'` (everything of his already waits) or `'empty'`. Events: `rebuilt {by, n, short}` and `rebuildHeld {by, n, paid}` (`paid` there is only the money held for the wrecks left waiting; the result's `paid` is all the press took). There is no cancel (the owner's decision). Waiting orders: `stepRebuilds(state, ev)`, called by `step()` after the shoppers move, does nothing unless something is ordered, then every `TUNE.rebuildEvery` (0.2 s of game time; a refused check costs a path search), oldest first: an order whose payer is gone or Head Office is cancelled and repaid; an order held at less than its full deal takes more of the payer's cash (`rebuildDeal(r, res + cash)`, never lowering `res`, so `res` is always exactly what some deal costs, and older wrecks are paid first), but never from a payer who is away (`!payer.on`: while away he neither earns nor pays), though what he already paid still goes back; then `putBack()` builds it if every `canPlace` check but the cash passes (`placeCheck(..., paid)`, shared with `canPlace`; `canPutBack(state, r)` gives the reason to the interface), with `rebuildDeal(r, res)` exactly as an immediate rebuild (owner = the payer, card price, free levels and booze kept; `spent` and `built` counted now). One `rebuilt` event per payer with `late: 1`. Held money is never lost: `releaseOrder(state, r)` in `state.js` gives `res` back to `by` whenever an ordered wreck stops being rubble any other way (built over, `clearRubble`; off the end of the 400-long list in `wreck()`; its payer taking Head Office; the game won or lost, `releaseAllOrders`, because the end screen counts cash). When an away player's grace period runs out, `expireAway` cancels his orders first, so the held money is shared out with the rest of his cash and no later refund can go to someone who has left; a player back within his grace period keeps his orders. Placing the same kind on a wreck he has paid for counts `res` as his cash in `canPlace`, and the `place` command repays any order on that wreck before charging, so he pays once. `heldFor(state, pid)` adds up a player's held money; the checksum mixes the total in. Old saves: `migrateSave` drops a player's `autoRebuild` and a wreck's `at` (both dead) at any version. On screen: `createRebuildPop()` in `src/ui/rebuild.js` floats a big REBUILD button over the bottom middle of the floor (clear of the entrances, the tills, DEB's bubble, the camera pad and the small map; game.js lifts it onto the held tool's card) while the player has a wreck, ordered or not. `rebuildStatus(state, me)` decides it without changing anything: wrecks not ordered (`n`, `cost`), whether B would order one (`can`, `why`), and those he has paid for (`held`, `paid`, `full`, `wait`: why the oldest waits, one path search); `rebuildWords` turns that into the three looks: lit (REBUILD 3 · $450), dimmed with the reason (Too broke. Need $X), and waiting (PAID $120 of $300 · 2 waiting, no nudge). The build bar's Rebuild button has the same idle / alert / waiting states, and the renderer puts a little orange cone on an ordered wreck (`drawRubble(..., ordered)`).

Opening the doors early: the `start` command calls `skipBuild()` in `step.js` before `beginWave()`. It plays the rest of the build timer through in one go (belts, machines, chutes, drying puddles; a build phase has no shoppers), so the store is exactly as if the players had waited, and emits one `early` event with the takings instead of a `sale` event per item. Anything new that runs during a build phase must be run there too.

Security switched off: `b.off` is the number of ticks a building stays out of action after a stray dog pees on it. `step()` counts it down; a building with `b.off` does not fire, stop or pitch.

Shoppers that affect other shoppers: a Day Drinker lying on a tile slows everyone crossing it; a Cosplayer takes the shots aimed at anyone within `TUNE.shield` tiles of him; an Exhibitionist stops shoppers whose definition has `gawks` (dads and teenagers); the TV Salesman rolls once per shopper using `SHOPPERS[kind].pitch = [chance to stop, chance to hurry]`. A shopper who stops first walks over to the stand (`s.sa` is the stand's tile while he does; `walkToStand()` in `step.js`), as near as he can get, and only then goes into `ST.WATCH`. A mom's child may run away once (twice for the Soccer Mom), never nearer than `TUNE.kidTill` tiles of walking from a till, and she will not take her refund without him; after a mom has gone down she has no cart and carries the child.

The boss: every fifth wave (`TUNE.bossEvery`) one boss walks in, as the last group `waveSpec()` returns. His definition is `BOSS` in `constants.js`, deliberately NOT in `SHOPPERS` or `SUPERS`, so nothing that lists the kinds of shopper picks him up by accident; `defOf(shopper)` and `shopperDef('boss')` return it. A boss shopper has `k: 'boss'`, `tr` (his tier: the wave divided by five, which also picks his title with `bossTitle(tier)`) and `pp` (ticks to his next pep talk, which gives shoppers near him some patience back). Nothing slows or stops him. Reaching a till he counts as `TUNE.bossRefunds` refunds (2 of a limit of 20; it was 5 of 40); worn out, he pays the store a bounty. He is not scaled by the patience multiplier (`patienceScaleAt`), and his refund is not multiplied by `perHead()`; his event's crowd is halved by `TUNE.crowdScale` like any other, on top of `TUNE.bossCrowd`. The other groups of a boss wave are `TUNE.bossCrowd` of their usual size. Events: `bossIn`, `pep`, `bossOut`, and `escape` with `n` (the refunds it counted). Head Office cannot buy him and he comes whatever was planned.

Holidays (0.3): `state.holiday` is a key of `HOLIDAYS` in `constants.js` (`xmas`, `thanksgiving`, `valentine`, `july4`, `halloween`, `stpat`, `easter`, `newyear`, `mardigras`), or absent for none (as in every save from before it, and every test game). `createGame({ holiday })` keeps a key on that list and drops anything else. No rule reads it: it only dresses the bosses up and gives DEB holiday lines, and a game plays out exactly the same with or without it. The simulation has no clock, so the calendar is outside it, in `src/content/holidays.js`: `holidayOn(date)` gives the holiday whose window (the 7 days before it, the day and the day after, by the date's own local calendar) contains that date, or null; where two windows overlap (Christmas and New Year's, Valentine's and Mardi Gras), the holiday whose day is nearer wins, and on a tie the one still to come. Easter is worked out for any year (Mardi Gras is 47 days before it), Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday of November. Only the host's start-up code asks it: `main.js` passes `holidayFor(urlValue)` (today's on the host's computer, or the `&holiday=` test option) to `new HostSession({ holiday })` and to `backToLobby({ holiday })`, so a new game, and a game restarted from the end screen, gets the holiday of the day it was made. Everyone else gets it with the state: a joined player sees the same outfit and hears the same lines, and a saved game keeps its own holiday when resumed. `HOLIDAY_INFO` there names each holiday and what the boss wears (`outfitText(key)`, "dressed as Santa", which the boss bar puts after his title and the handbook's boss card under "Dress code").

The cards (0.3.01): every game from the menu is dealt five cards that change the rules: two bad for the players, two good, one neither, revealed in the order bad, good, bad, good, odd. The design of every card is the lead's (`.claude/briefs/deck.md`, not tracked); their numbers are `CARDS` in `constants.js`, `DEALABLE` lists the ones whose rules exist (only those are dealt; a forced hand may hold any), `CARDS_APART` the pairs never dealt together (two per-kind cards only for the same kind), `CARD_SKIP_KINDS` the kinds a per-kind card never picks (the Exhibitionist, the Card Trader). `src/sim/cards.js` holds the rest:
- `state.cards` is the hand, plain card ids in the order they are revealed; a per-kind card carries its kind after a colon (`'hardy:karen'`). Absent means no cards: every save from before, the tutorial, the URL test options and every test game. Cards never apply in a training game. `createGame({ cards: true })` deals with `rand()` (`dealCards(state)`, which returns the hand) and sets `state.dealT = 0`: the deal is still to come. A list of ids is a forced hand (`cleanHand` drops anything that is not a card, a per-kind card without a kind it may pick, and repeats), with no deal phase.
- The deal is the phase `'cards'`, between the lobby and the first build phase: a game made with `cards: true` and no lobby starts in it; from the lobby, `step()` goes to it instead of `'build'` when everyone is ready (no `begun` yet). Nothing runs in it but the clock: `state.tick` and the grace period of a player who drops out go on, and `stepDeal()` reveals the cards. Its timing is `TUNE.cardLead`, `cardSecs` and `cardPause`, in seconds at normal speed: `state.dealT` counts sixtieths of a tick and each step adds 60 / speed (a whole number at every speed in `SPEEDS`), so the deal takes the same time at any speed. `revealTick(i)` is the tick (at normal speed) card i appears, `dealLength(n)` the whole deal, `dealTicks(state)` how far in it is, and `cardsShown(state)` how many are face up (0 in the lobby, all after the deal and in a game with a forced hand). Events: `deal {n}` on the first step of the phase, `card {i, id}` as each is revealed, and at the end `begun`, with the game in `'build'`, `phaseT` -1, exactly as from the lobby; `dealT` is then removed. The command `{ c: 'skipCards', on }` sets the sender's `player.skip`; once every connected store player has it, the remaining cards are revealed at once (their `card` events still sent) and the build phase begins. A player who drops out stops counting, so he cannot hold it up, and the clock ends it anyway. During the deal every command but `skipCards`, `speed` and `role` is refused with the reason `'cards'` (`canPlace` and `canUpgrade` give it too); `skipCards` outside it is refused with `'phase'`. A game in the deal is not saved (`worthSaving` wants a build phase or a wave).

The successor makes a `HostSession` from its own copy of the state with `takeOver: true` and opens the room again under the same code, retrying until the old host's code is released; the others retry joining it. Players are matched to their old places by name. The game screen is torn down and started again on the new session (`destroy()` on what `startGame` returns).

Saving: `src/ui/save.js` keeps one save in the host browser's `localStorage` (`keepSaved(session)` writes the whole state every few seconds, at each wave's end and when the page closes; `readSave()` gives it back with a summary for the menu, or null if there is none or it does not look right). `actions.resume()` in `src/main.js` makes a `HostSession` from it with `resume: true`, which seats the host by name and treats everyone else as away, money intact, until he rejoins. The state carries its version (`state.v`, `STATE_VERSION` in `state.js`, 4 since 0.2.09); `readSave` accepts this version and the one before, and resuming runs `migrateSave(state)` first, once: a version-3 save (refund limit 40 plus 10 a player) gets today's limit for its players and its refunds scaled by the same ratio, rounded down, so it keeps the share of its limit it had used. Only real games are saved: not training, not the lobby, not finished games, not the URL test options. (A game that was won and then carried on as endless is a game in progress again, and is saved from the moment of the `endless` event.) `src/ui/record.js` keeps, per difficulty, the best sales event reached in an endless game in the same browser storage; the lost screen and the menu show it.

Employee Handbook and README lists: `src/content/facts.js` works out, from the rules, the numbers people are shown: a shopper's patience in any wave, how many turn up, what its super version changes, and a row per level of each upgrade track for every building (`trackRows(kind, track)`). `src/ui/handbook.js` is the in-game handbook (three tabs: Known offenders, Factory, Security; opened from the menu, the book button in the top bar and the ? screen; in a game it uses that game's difficulty and player count). Its tables by sales event go up to the longest game the length drop-down offers (`LONGEST` in `facts.js`, the biggest of `SETTINGS.waves`), whatever the game's own length; an endless game's go further (`sampleWaves`, `bossWaves`). `src/ui/portrait.js` draws a shopper with the renderer's own code (`drawShopper` in `src/render/shoppers.js`; its last argument says what a watching shopper is looking at, and `noBar` leaves the patience bar out). `tools/readme.mjs` writes the version, the store designs and the shoppers into `README.md` between `<!-- gen:... -->` markers and, with `--shots`, retakes the pictures in `docs/img/` (maps from `tools/render-harness.html`, shoppers from `tools/portrait.html`). `test/readme.test.js` fails if the README is out of date and checks the handbook's numbers against the simulation.

Touch wording: lessons and help are written once, for mouse and keyboard. `src/content/touch.js` rewords them for touch screens (`worded(text)`), and a test fails if any lesson or help text still names a key or the mouse afterwards. Add a rule there when a new lesson mentions a key.

Installing: `manifest.webmanifest`, the icons in `icons/` (photographs of `tools/icon.html`) and `sw.js` let phones offer "Install app". `sw.js` deliberately caches nothing, so the newest version always loads.

Boss bar: `src/ui/bossbar.js` is the strip over the top of the floor that announces the next boss during the build phase and shows his title and patience while he is in the store. It does the same, in purple, for a Midnight Release: the head count before it, a countdown to the mob, then how many of the mob are left. It floats over the floor and has a fixed height, so it never resizes anything.

Chat: `session.chat(text)` sends a line to everyone and `session.on('chat', fn)` receives `{ pid, name, color, text }`. The host tidies and rate-limits it; it is not part of the game state. `src/ui/chat.js` is the box.

Touch: `src/ui/input.js` treats `pointerType` 'touch' and 'pen' (a pen since 0.3.03) separately from the mouse. Two fingers always pan and pinch-zoom and never build; one finger with nothing held drags the view, and a tap selects a building. Since 0.3.01 a finger places one thing at a time: with any building but the belt held, or the Remove or Upgrade tool, the preview (`view.hover`) follows the finger while it is down, with dashed lines along the tile's row and column (`view.aim`) because the finger hides the tile, and the building is placed (removed, or its sheet opened) once, on the tile the finger lifts from; lifted off the map or over any interface element (`elementFromPoint` is not the canvas) nothing happens. Only the belt still lays a line when dragged (tiles are laid as the finger moves). A second finger on the canvas while the first is placing cancels it: nothing more is built when either lifts (belt tiles a drag has already laid stay; the one under the finger is not laid), the held building stays selected, and the two fingers pan and pinch. `input.placing` gives the placing finger's position, or null. Since 0.3.03 (the owner's requests) a building's preview is lifted above the finger placing it, and it goes where the preview is: once the finger has moved `TAP_SLOP_PX` (8) from where it went down (`createPress`; time plays no part, and once up it stays up for that touch), the footprint is centred `liftPx(zoom)` above it (1.5 tiles, clamped to 56-110 CSS px, eased in over 140 ms; `liftedSpot`, `liftedAnchor`, which keeps the footprint inside the map); a finger that never moves that far places on the tapped tile. The lifted point must be on the floor itself (`elementFromPoint`), or nothing is placed. While lifted, `view.aim` is false and `view.lift` gives the finger in tiles, from which the renderer draws a thin line up to the preview (`drawLift`); the Remove and Upgrade tools, the belt and a resting finger keep `view.aim`. A building can also be dragged out of the bar: `input.watchBar(el, { onPick })` (called by `buildbar.js` on the bar's scroller) listens for a finger or pen pressed on a button with `data-k` of a building, and, on the window in the capture phase, for its moves: `barGesture(dx, dy, outOfBar)` decides 'pick' (12 px mostly up, or onto the floor), 'scroll' (mostly sideways: the bar has `touch-action: pan-x`, so the browser scrolls it and cancels the pointer) or 'wait'. A picked building (`input.carrying`, `input.placingKind`; `view.tool` is it meanwhile, `input.tool` never changes) is placed once where its lifted preview is when the finger lifts; a `pointercancel`, a finger landing on the canvas, or losing focus ends it with nothing placed; the click the button may get afterwards is swallowed. In `input.tick`, a lifted placement within `EDGE.bandPx` (48) of an edge of the canvas pans the view by `edgeVelocity` (up to 8 tiles a second, deeper is faster; for a bar drag the bottom edge counts only once the finger has been above that band) and the preview's tile is recomputed. The numbers and decisions are in `src/ui/lift.js`, pure and tested in Node. `src/ui/touch.js` adds the on-screen buttons for what the keyboard does, the sheet of actions for a tapped building, and, while a finger is placing, the placing aid: a 64-pixel Rotate button (only for a building that turns) and the hint "Tap the floor with another finger to cancel", halfway up the side of the floor away from the finger. Its Rotate button, like the one in the bar, acts on `pointerdown`, because a second finger's tap may never become a click; the hint has `pointer-events: none`, so a finger on it lands on the floor. All of these appear only on `(pointer: coarse)` devices. Phone and tablet layouts are one block at the end of `src/style.css`, switched on by screen size; desktop layouts must not change.

Settings: `SETTINGS` in `constants.js` lists what a host may choose: the number of waves, each player's starting cash, and a difficulty (which multiplies shopper patience, including the boss's and the babies', and income: sale prices, the end-of-event bonus and the boss's bounty). Normal is the baseline, x1 and x1 by definition, so every number in `constants.js` is Normal's and the handbook and README show them as they are; the other levels are round numbers relative to it (Easy x0.75 patience and x1.25 income, Hard x1.3 and x0.85, Brutal x1.65 and x0.7). Money is rounded to whole dollars after the multiplier, so a base price can be a decimal (a Gadget's 7.7 pays $8 on Normal); anything shown to a player must be the rounded amount. A training game uses no difficulty: its shoppers get base patience divided by `TUNE.trainingPatienceDivisor` (0.9), the numbers its lessons were written for before 0.2.10 made Normal the baseline. They live in the state (`state.waves`, `state.startCash`, `state.difficulty`), are given to `createGame({ settings })`, and can be changed in the lobby with `{ c: 'settings', ... }`. Read them from the state, never from `TUNE`. `src/ui/settings.js` is the set of drop-downs the menu and the lobby share (since 0.3.01 also Cards: On / Off, see the cards above; `cleanSettings` gives `cardsOn`, true unless it is false).

Lobby: a game made with `createGame({ lobby: true })` starts in phase `'lobby'`. Nothing runs and nothing can be built; the host (player 0) may send `{ c: 'map', design, size }`, which swaps the store with `setMap()` and clears everyone's `ready`; each player sends `{ c: 'ready', on }`; `step()` moves to `'build'` when every connected player is ready (to `'cards'`, the deal, first in a game with cards still to deal; see the cards above). Games started from the menu use it; the URL test options do not unless `&lobby=1` is given. `src/ui/lobby.js` is the panel.

Head Office: `src/sim/office.js`. One player may switch `side` to `'office'` and buy the coming wave from a budget instead of building (`state.office`, the `side` and `plan` commands). His plan is turned into the same list of groups `waveSpec()` produces, with an entrance per group. He is left out of everything that counts store players (`shareCount`, income, the review).

Path maps: there are four (`NORMAL`, `HOP` for dads, `SMASH` for gym bros, `GHOST` for runaway children, who are stopped only by the store's walls), plus `getFieldTo(state, tile)` for a mom running to where her child waits. All are cached until `state.gridVer` changes. `modeOf(kind)` is a kind's own map; `routeOf(shopper)` is the one he walks by right now (a gym bro who cannot hit anything at the moment walks `NORMAL`, see below).

Gym bros' limit (0.2.12, the owner's numbers): a gym bro lands at most `smashMax` hits in one visit (2; the Roid Rager 3) and rests `smashWait` seconds after each (10; the Roid Rager 5); both are in `TUNE` with the Roid Rager's own in `SHOPPERS.gymbro.sup`, read with `tv(def, key)`. Every hit he lands counts, even one a durability level soaks up. The shopper fields are `hn` (hits landed) and `hw` (ticks of rest left, counted down whatever he is doing); a missing field reads as 0. `smashReady(shopper)` in `path.js` says whether he may hit now; while he may not (resting, or spent), he hits nothing and walks the `NORMAL` map like anyone else, so he never stands at a crate waiting. `smashEvery` is still the time between attempts that did not land. Standing on a crate tile still flattens it at once, without using a hit. Because he may become an ordinary walker at any moment, `canPlace` checks gym bros on the `NORMAL` map: nobody may wall one in with crates.

Coordinates: the grid is `state.w` x `state.h` tiles. A tile index is `y * state.w + x`. Shopper positions are in tiles as decimals; the centre of tile (3, 5) is (3.5, 5.5). Directions are 0 east, 1 south, 2 west, 3 north.

## Sessions (`src/net/session.js`)
The host's browser owns the real state. Clients send commands. The host sends thirty small "advance" messages a second (one per step at normal speed): its step count, the commands it applied (each with the step it took effect at and who gave it), and a checksum. Clients replay them on their own copy, aiming to run three steps (about a tenth of a second) behind the host, and ask for a fresh copy if the checksum differs. A full copy is sent only when a player joins or leaves, after `fastForward`, on a mismatch, when a client falls more than 3 seconds behind, and every 10 seconds as a safety net. Messages over 48 KB (15 KB over the internet, the limit of the PeerJS library) are split and rejoined in `transports.js`. 'say', 'review' and 'report' reach a client when his replay reaches that moment. Game speed: `state.speed` is one of `SPEEDS` in `constants.js` (1, 2, 3 and, since 0.2.14, 5; no 4), set by any player with `{ c: 'speed', n }`; the host runs that many steps in the time of one, and the per-frame step limits (`HOST_MAX_STEPS`, the client's `MAX_STEPS`) grow with the top speed. While the game is paused (`state.pause`) both sides go on counting and sending steps as usual, so messages, copies, joins and chat all keep working, but each step changes nothing; the checksum includes the pause (only while there is one, so an unpaused game sums as before), and the host does not ask the director for its once-a-second `idle()` lines while paused (it asks when `state.tick` is a whole second, and the tick stands still). `fastForward` on a paused game runs its steps and returns. Never change `session.state` on the host directly while players are connected: go through `send()`. The user interface talks to a session and never to the simulation's state-changing functions directly:

| Member | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `session.state` | current state; `null` on a client until it has joined |
| `session.me` | this player's id (index into `state.players`) |
| `session.isHost` | true for `HostSession` |
| `session.send(cmd)` | issue a command |
| `session.sendCursor(x, y)` | tell others where you point, in tiles (decimals); throttle to about 10 a second |
| `session.on(type, fn)` | listen for a message; returns an unsubscribe function |
| `session.drainFx()` | simulation events since the last call, for effects. **Only the renderer calls this.** |
| `session.alpha()` | 0..1 progress between ticks; draw shoppers at `x + vx * alpha` |
| `session.start()` / `stop()` | run on a timer |
| `session.fastForward(n)` | host only: run n ticks at once (screenshots, tests) |
| `session.backToLobby({seed, director, holiday, cards})` | host only, once the game is won or lost: replace it with a fresh game in the lobby (same store and settings, the players still connected, in their old order) and send it to every client over the open connections. Returns false while the game is still on |

Messages delivered through `session.on`: `ready`, `lobby`, `say`, `review`, `report`, `players`, `nope`, `cursor`, `closed`. Their fields are listed at the top of `src/net/session.js`.

Going round again: in a multiplayer game the end screen's button reads "Back to the lobby" for the host (clients see that the host decides, and a "Quit to the menu" button). The host's `backToLobby()` sends each client a `lobby {me, n, s}` message: the new state, his id in it, and the step it starts at. The host moves its step count on by one first, so everything about the old game carries a smaller count; a client drops any `adv`, `snap` or `at` older than that step, and throws away whatever of the old game was still queued. Both sides then emit `lobby`, and the game screen takes down the end screen and shows the lobby as it does when the phase is `lobby`. The room code and link stay the same because no connection is closed. Clients cannot ask for it; there is no such command.

Creating sessions:
```js
import { HostSession, ClientSession } from './net/session.js';
import { createDirector } from './content/director.js';
import { hostOnline, joinOnline, hostLocal, joinLocal, makeRoomCode } from './net/transports.js';

// Solo, or the host of a multiplayer game:
const session = new HostSession({ name, director: createDirector() });
const room = await hostOnline(code, (conn) => session.accept(conn));   // omit for solo

// A friend joining:
const conn = await joinOnline(code);          // rejects with a readable Error
const session = new ClientSession(conn, name); // wait for the 'ready' message before drawing
```
`hostLocal` / `joinLocal` do the same between tabs of one browser, for testing without the internet. `hostOnline` / `joinOnline` need `vendor/peerjs.min.js` loaded by a plain `<script>` tag before the modules.

## Renderer (`src/render/renderer.js`)
```js
const renderer = createRenderer(canvas, session);
renderer.resize();                 // after the canvas element changes size
renderer.tileAt(clientX, clientY); // -> {x, y} tile under a mouse position, or null
renderer.draw(view);               // once per animation frame

// The camera belongs to the renderer. Maps can be much bigger than the screen.
renderer.cam;                      // read-only { x, y, zoom }: view centre in tiles, CSS pixels per tile
renderer.pan(dxTiles, dyTiles);
renderer.centerOn(xTiles, yTiles);
renderer.zoomBy(factor, clientX, clientY); // the tile under that point stays put
renderer.fit();                    // show the whole map
renderer.viewRect();               // -> { x0, y0, x1, y1 } tiles on screen
```
The camera never zooms out past the whole map or in past 72 pixels a tile, and never leaves the map by more than a tile. It starts on the whole map if tiles would be at least 24 pixels, otherwise at 30 pixels a tile. `src/ui/input.js` drives it (WASD, wheel, middle-drag, F, H). While the game is paused the renderer holds the fraction between ticks (`session.alpha()`) at its last value, so `F.time` stands still and shoppers do not jog on the spot.

Pause on screen (0.3.02): the pause button sits in the top bar just before the speed buttons (`.hud-clock` in `hud.js`: two bars, a play triangle on yellow while paused; under ☰ on a phone, beside the speeds), and `P` (`togglePause` in `input.js`, not while typing) does the same. The top bar's `fit()` may narrow it at step 4 but never drops it. While paused, `game.js` dims the floor (`.pause-veil`, which lets clicks through, so a click with a building held gets DEB's `paused` refusal) and shows the PAUSED banner over it, naming who paused; clicking the banner carries on. The root gets `.is-paused`, which greys out the build bar's buttons. The help screen and the README list the key.

`createMinimap(canvas, session, renderer)` in `src/render/minimap.js` returns `{ draw(), tileAt(clientX, clientY) }`: a small picture of the whole store with the visible part outlined. The game screen shows it when the store does not fit on screen.
`view` is supplied by the UI each frame:
```js
{
  tool: 'belt',          // a key of BUILD, or 'remove' or 'upgrade', or null when nothing is selected
  dir: 0,                // facing for the placement preview, 0-3
  track: 'rng',          // optional: the upgrade track a preview is about (only 'rng' changes what is drawn);
                         // set from the open sheet: the upgrade button under the mouse or with keyboard focus
  selected: { x, y },    // optional: the building whose sheet is open (its anchor tile), or null; its reach is drawn
  hover: { x, y },       // tile under the mouse, or null
  aim: false,            // true while a finger is placing something under itself: dashed lines along the hover tile's row and column
  lift: { x, y },        // or null: the finger placing a lifted building (0.3.03), in tiles with decimals; a line to the preview
  cursors: Map,          // player id -> { x, y } in tiles (decimals): other players' pointers
  route: true,           // draw the route shoppers will walk, and the dads' short cut (always, since 0.4)
  routeFaint: false,     // draw it a little fainter (shoppers on the floor and nobody planning)
  floor: 0,              // 0.4: the floor looked at, 0 the shop, 1 The Back (input.floor; never in the state)
  marks: [{ x, y, label }], // tiles to highlight with a pulsing outline and arrow (tutorial)
}
```
Two floors (0.4, stage 2b). `src/render/back.js` (`createBackArt`) draws The Back: its belts with the shop floor's belt sprites (`drawBeltTile` in `buildings.js`, ends and corners worked out from the Back's own layout and `beltShape` in `back.js`), the items riding them (pre-rendered: junk, and five supplies at four tiers, the tier a ring: none, bronze, silver, gold), moved on between ticks only as far as they will really get (so an item waiting at a belt's end does not creep and jump back), round corners on the curve, and the parts ported from `docs/mockups/0.4/mockups.js` (Splitter, Overpass with the north-south lane on a bridge over the east-west one, Unpaid Intern with what he holds, Returns Drop, Clearance Bin with its pay floating up from the `binned` event). Only visible tiles are visited. On the shop floor (`view.floor` 0) The Back is drawn into an offscreen layer, darkened and laid over the floor at about a quarter strength, under the buildings. In The Back (`view.floor` 1) the floor picture is darkened (walls and shelves still read: they go all the way down), The Back's parts are drawn at full strength, then the routes faintly, the shop's buildings and shoppers in a layer at about 30%, and a dashed outline round each security building; the shop's effects and floating money are left out. The placement preview there uses `canPlaceBack`. The interface: `input.floor` / `input.setFloor()`, switched with Tab or the floor switch over the bottom right of the floor (`.floor-switch`, "CHECK IN THE BACK" / "BACK TO THE FLOOR"); switching drops whatever is held. On floor 1 the build bar shows The Back's parts (`BACK_KEYS` in `input.js`, keys 1 to 5, help texts `BACK_HOW` in `help.js`) and Remove; Upgrade and Rebuild belong to the shop floor (B still rebuilds). Every place, remove and rotate sent from floor 1 carries `f: 1`.
A selected building's reach (0.3.02, the owner's request): while a building's sheet is open (`src/ui/touch.js`; clicked or tapped with nothing held or with the Upgrade tool), `game.js` sets `view.selected` to it (`touchUi.selected`) and `view.track` to the sheet's upgrade button under the mouse or with keyboard focus (`touchUi.track`: delegated `pointerover`/`pointerout`/`focusin`/`focusout` on the buttons' container, each upgrade button carrying `data-track`, because the sheet replaces its buttons after a purchase and a replaced button never reports the mouse leaving; a finger never sets it). `selectedReach(state, view)` in `src/render/reach.js` (pure, tested in Node) says what to draw: the building's current reach in the `'sel'` style (a solid white edge over a dark one; the laser's beam strip the same way), and, while `view.track` is `'rng'` and Range is not at its top, the reach after one more level in the dashed gold `'next'` style on top. Only the range track changes reach (`statsOf`), so the other tracks change nothing on the floor. The hover preview (`drawPreview`) leaves the selected building's reach out (`drawnBySelection`), so it is drawn once, with the mouse, a finger or the Upgrade tool on it. When the reach would be under the sheet, `keepInSight` in `game.js` moves the view up once on a phone (as far as the map's edge allows) and otherwise opens the sheet at the top of the floor (`.touch-sheet.at-top`); on a big screen with a mouse it never moves the view.

Holiday outfits: the renderer sets `F.dress` to `state.holiday` every frame, and `drawShopper` draws the boss in that holiday's outfit (`DRESS` in `src/render/shoppers.js`: one entry per key of `HOLIDAYS`, in the same order; the sprite cache keys his pictures by it). An outfit may recolour his jacket, trousers and tie and draws its own parts at fixed moments of his drawing (behind him, his legs, over his trousers, over his jacket, in the Regional Manager's free hand, over his tie and lanyard, on his head); his rank's hair, props, lanyard and name plate stay, so the four ranks can still be told apart. It is drawing only: his size, position and the rules are untouched. `drawBossTag` lifts the name plate above the outfit's hat (`hat`). `shopperPortrait('boss', { dress })` draws a dressed portrait for the handbook.

The renderer draws the world (any map size and design, only the part in view), the marked floor from `state.zones` (painted into the cached floor; holding a machine makes its patches glow, and a preview off its floor carries a caption such as "PRESS SHOP ONLY"), the placement and upgrade previews (green or red using `canPlace` and `canUpgrade`; the whole footprint of a bigger building; range circles, and for a Barcode Laser the strip its beam covers), upgrade badges (the number is `levelOf`), cracks and bandages on a building gym bros have hit, the puddle on security a dog has switched off, rubble, effects from `session.drainFx()`, and other players' pointers. Entrances carry no names: an entrance belongs to the store, not to the player whose arrival opened it. It does not handle input and does not touch the DOM outside its canvas.

## Content (`src/content`)
- `lines.js` holds every line of dialogue. Add jokes there, not in code. A plain string is a common line; `u('...')` makes it uncommon (about a third as likely) and `r('...')` rare (about a tenth). The rules for adding lines are at the top of the file.
- `director.js` runs on the host, turns simulation events into `say`, `review` and `report` messages, and decides how often DEB speaks. Its output goes to every player, so everyone hears the same line with the same name in it. Each kind of remark is a topic in `TOPICS`: some are always said; the rest get less likely, and further apart, every time DEB has commented on that topic in this game. A line that has been used has its weight divided by `TALK.repeatPenalty` for the rest of the game. All the numbers are in `TALK`.
- Holidays (0.3): `HOLIDAY_LINES` in `lines.js` has, for each key of `HOLIDAYS`, four banks: `in` (the boss walks in), `out` (he storms off), `through` (he gets his refund) and `pause` (a joke in a build pause). In a game with `state.holiday`, the first walk-in, exit and refund of the game get a line from that holiday's bank instead of the usual one, and after that `TALK.holidayBoss` (60%) of them do; those lines name him with "the {k}" like the usual ones. Once each build pause, `TALK.holidayDelay` seconds after it starts and only while more than `TALK.holidayLast` seconds are left (clear of the friends' jabs and the ten-second warning), DEB may tell a `pause` joke: the `holiday` topic, a joke topic, so it fades over the game like any other (about four in a 20-event game, half of them in the first five pauses). Not in the training shift, and nothing at all with no holiday.
- The three friends: `FRIENDS` in `lines.js` has lines written for players named Bobb, Phantasm and Tusker (matched by exact name, ignoring capitals). They are used only when a player with that name is connected: a third of the times a line would name him, and now and then unprompted between waves.
- `nopeLine(why, name)` gives the line shown to one player when his command is refused.
- Pausing (0.3.02): `LINES.paused` (8 lines) and `LINES.resumed` (4), each naming the player who did it, are the note topics `paused` and `resumed` in `TOPICS`: one line per `pause` event at most, the first always, then fading like any note; their gap is counted in ticks of play, which stand still while paused, so pressing P over and over gets no more lines. `NOPE.paused` answers a command refused while paused.

## Sound (`src/audio`)
`createSound()` in `src/audio/sound.js` makes every sound with the Web Audio API and speaks DEB's lines with the browser's speech synthesis. `src/main.js` loads it with a dynamic import, so the game still runs (silently) if it fails. It listens to the session's `fx`, `say`, `review`, `report` and `players` messages; the interface calls `sound.play(name)` for its own sounds (`select`, `place`, `remove`, `rotate`, `upgrade`, `rebuild`, `chat`, `nope`, `click`, `tutorial`). Frequent sounds vary on every play (three versions each, with pitch, length and timbre nudged by a random seed), and each kind of shopper has its own voice in `src/audio/shoppers.js`. `src/audio/crowd.js` holds the rules for many sounds at once: spacing, thinning, lowering the level as they pile up, merging a mass walkout or a run of sales into one sound, and a cap on how much is played. It contains no audio code, so the test page runs the same rules. Every effect is one entry in the table in `src/audio/effects.js` (the sounds added in 0.2 are built in `src/audio/newfx.js`, the boss's and endless mode's in `src/audio/boss.js`, the Midnight Release's and a ripped pack's in `src/audio/trader.js`, the deal of the cards' in `src/audio/cards.js` (played by the interface with `sound.play(name, opts)`); `bossOut` carries the sound when a boss is driven out, so `sound.js` drops the `left` that arrives with it). The `pause` event plays `pause` (a record scratch and a turntable winding down) or `unpause` (spinning back up) for every player; the store music, the only sound that loops, sinks to `PAUSED` (0.3) of its level while `state.pause` is set (`pauseDuck` in `sound.js`, also asked whenever the music is scheduled, so a game joined or resumed while paused is quiet too). Nobody can check sound by ear in an automated run, so `tools/sound-test.html` renders each effect offline and measures it (silent, clipping, clicks, too long); keep it at ALL PASS and add new sounds to the table.

Sound levels (0.3.03). Each player sets his own sound in the menu behind the speaker button (`createSoundMenu` in `src/ui/hud.js`): the overall Volume (the master) and a level from 0 to 1 for each of six groups, Money, Shoppers, Security and machines, Announcements, Store music and DEB's voice (`GROUPS` in `src/audio/groups.js`, which also reads and keeps the settings and has no audio code, so Node tests run it). Every effect belongs to one group by `GROUP_OF`, beside the table in `effects.js` (a test fails if one has none; DEB's blips are in `voice`, with her speech). `createChain` in `mix.js` gives each group a bus feeding the effects bus, and the music a level after the gain that ducks it, so a moved slider changes a sound already playing (50 ms glide) and the ducks multiply with the music's level. `sound.js` does not build a sound at all for a group at 0 (nor anything at master 0), and it never reaches the crowd gate, so the other groups are thinned as before; the music stops being scheduled at 0; DEB speaks at master times her level. `sound.get(key)` / `sound.set(key, value)` take `volume` and the six group keys as numbers (clamped); `sound.on(group)` says whether a group can be heard; `sound.toggle(key)` is the speaker mark beside a row (0, or back to its last level above 0, which is saved too); `sound.preview(group)` plays one short sample of a group at its level when a slider is let go (one at a time; DEB says a line of `SOUND_CHECK` in `lines.js`). The settings stay in the player's browser (localStorage `returnsDept.sound`), never in the game state. Saves from before 0.3.03 held three switches: `sfx: false` becomes the four effect groups at 0, `music: false` and `voice: false` their level at 0; only levels are written back. With the defaults (everything at 1, master 0.7) every sound comes out exactly as before; `tools/sound-test.html` checks that, and that each group at 0 is silent and at 0.5 half as loud.

## The Back (0.4, built in stages; `PLAN.md` says which stages are done)
From 0.4 the factory is a second floor under the shop, **The Back**. It makes no money. It turns returned junk into supplies and delivers them to the security buildings above, which are stronger while stocked. This section is the contract for it. Where it disagrees with the older sections above, this section is the target and the older text describes code that has not been replaced yet.

### Two floors, one grid
- `state.back` is a flat array like `state.grid`, the same size and indexing: a Back part or 0 per tile. `state.backVer` goes up whenever a part is added, removed or turned, and derived data is cached against it (as path maps are against `gridVer`).
- A tile of The Back can hold a part unless `state.tiles[i] === '#'`: the store's walls and shelves go all the way down. Tills and entrances have open floor under them. Nothing in The Back blocks shoppers and no route check is needed to place a part.
- Back parts are kinds in `BUILD` with `back: true` and group `'back'`. They are bought, owned (`o`), removed and refunded like any building. `SHOP_KINDS` lists every other kind; the build bar, hotkeys, handbook and tests that list the shop's buildings read it. Of the cards, only shrinkflation and staffdiscount change a Back part's price (the group cards and free levels never touch it); a price a card changed is kept as `c`, as on the shop floor.
- The `place`, `remove` and `rotate` commands take `f: 1` to mean The Back (no `f`, or 0, means the shop floor; any other `f` is refused with `'bad'`). `canPlaceBack(state, k, x, y, pid, d)` is `canPlace`'s partner. A shop kind in The Back, or a Back kind on the shop floor, is refused with `'kind'`, and so is placing a Returns Drop (`fixed: true` in `BUILD`); removing one is refused with `'fixed'`. A belt laid on a belt turns it, free. Events: `placed` and `removed` with `f: 1`.
- Which floor a player is looking at is not in the state. It is local to his screen (`view.floor`, 0 shop, 1 The Back), switched with Tab. The floor he is not on is drawn faintly (about 25%).
- The engine lives in `src/sim/back.js`. `stepBack(state, ev)` runs once per tick, in the build pause and during events alike, before the security buildings fire (and, until `skipBuild` goes, through the time it skips). Its event is `binned {i, v, by}`: a Clearance Bin on tile i was paid v for a supply. Besides the functions named below it exports `JUNK`, `dropTicks`, `newPart`, `overpassDirs(state, i)` and `beltShape(state, i)` (for drawing); `makeDrops` is in `maps.js`.

### Items
- An item is a small whole number. `1` is returned junk. A supply is `10 * type + tier`, type 1 Bottled Spite (track `dmg`), 2 Rage Cola (`spd`), 3 Extension Cord (`rng`), 4 Duct Tape (`dur`), 5 Razor Wire (`wire`), tier 1 Store Brand, 2 Name Brand, 3 Premium, 4 Platinum. So Premium Rage Cola is 23. `back.js` exports `itemCode`, `itemType`, `itemTier`.
- Nothing stores items except belts in motion, the one-item hands and slots named below, and the dumbwaiters. There is no warehouse.

### Belts (`conv`, shown as "Belt")
- A belt tile is `{k:'conv', o, d, it:[code, pos, code, pos, ...]}`: the items on it, front one first. `pos` is a whole number from 0 (entering) to `BACK.sub` (leaving), so there are no fractions to drift between browsers.
- Numbers, all in `BACK` in `constants.js`: `sub` 240 units per tile; `gap` 80, the least distance between two items, so a tile holds three; `step` 16 units a tick, which is 2 tiles a second; `end` = `sub - gap / 2`, where an item waits when it cannot go on. The same table has `internSecs`, `dropSecs`, `dropMul` (a guess, to tune) and `binPay`.
- Movement must not depend on tile order. Lanes are stepped **downstream first**: a lane is stepped after the lane it hands into, so a full line moves as one and no item moves twice in a tick. The order is derived from the layout and cached against `backVer` (never stored in the state). A ring of belts is broken at its lowest tile index: what is handed into that tile (a lane or a Splitter) before its turn stays put that tick. So that a ring with no free space still circulates at full speed, the one hand-over across the break assumes the last item of the lane it joins moves a `step` on that tick; if the ring is held up and it does not, those two items sit up to one `step` closer than `gap` until the ring moves again (never closer, and never more than that one pair).
- Turning a belt (or anything next to it) can leave two items briefly closer than `gap` across a join, until the line moves on. An Overpass lane's direction is read from the layout again whenever a neighbour changes, so after an edit the items on it may run the other way.
- **Corners.** A belt with nothing feeding it from behind and exactly one feeder at one side is a corner (`beltShape`: 1 fed from its right, -1 from its left, 0 straight, the shop floor's numbers). A feeder is any part whose output goes into the tile: a belt facing it, an Overpass lane running into it, a Splitter's way, a Returns Drop (later a machine's front); not an Unpaid Intern. Worked out from the layout and cached against `backVer`. Items from a corner's feeding side come on exactly as from behind.
- A front item heading into a belt as if from behind (straight on, or round a corner) may advance to `sub + (that belt's last pos - gap)`, which keeps the gap across the join. Once past `sub` it is handed over with the overshoot as its new `pos`, so it travels the same distance a tile on a corner as on a straight. Heading into anything else it advances to `end` and is offered each tick.
- A belt takes items from behind and from both sides, never head-on. A merge (a feeder from behind and one at a side, or one at each side) takes side items at `sub / 2` if no item is within `gap` of that spot. A belt fed from behind and from the side gives way to neither: whichever lane is stepped first that tick wins, and the order is fixed.
- The step is written as a function over one lane array, so a second lane (`it2`, the two-sided belts of Factorio) can be added later without a rewrite.

### Handing over
- Everything that passes an item on uses two functions in `back.js`: `wants(state, i, code, dir, over)` (would tile `i` take this item, arriving travelling in direction `dir`, right now?) and `give(state, i, code, dir, over)`, which does it. `over` (default 0) is how far past the tile's edge the item already is: a belt handing on as if from behind passes its overshoot; everything else 0. `wants` has no side effects. All hand-overs go through them, so a new part only has to answer those two.
- **Splitter** `{k:'split', o, d, h, r}`: takes one item from behind into `h` (0 when empty), and each tick offers it to the tiles in front, to its left and to its right, starting after the one that took the last item (`r`), skipping any that do not want it. One belt in, up to three out, shared evenly among the ways that are open.
- **Overpass** `{k:'over', o, a:[...], b:[...]}`: two belt lanes that do not mix, `a` running east-west and `b` north-south. Which way each runs is read from the layout (the neighbour that feeds into it; east and south win a tie), not stored.
- **Unpaid Intern** `{k:'intern', o, d, h, t}`: stands between two tiles. With an empty hand he takes from the belt behind him the front-most item that the tile in front of him `wants`, holds it for `BACK.internSecs`, then gives it (onto a belt: at 0 from behind, otherwise at the middle). He takes from belts only, not Overpass lanes.
- **Returns Drop** `{k:'drop', d, t, lv}`: fixed by the map, owned by nobody, cannot be removed, can be turned by anyone. Only a game made with a map has them (the bare default map, used by training and most tests, has none for now; an old save gets an empty Back). `makeDrops` in `maps.js` puts one under each Loading Bay patch of the old marked floor, working the patches out itself (tiles up to two apart are one patch, so a patch a shelf cuts in two gets one drop), on the patch tile nearest its middle, facing the store's middle from top to bottom, or the next way that is not a wall. Every `dropTicks(lv)` (`BACK.dropSecs`, times `BACK.dropMul` for each cash level `lv`, the `dropUp` command of a later stage) it puts one junk on the tile in front if that tile wants it, and waits while it does not.
- **Clearance Bin** `{k:'bin', o}`: takes anything from any side. Junk pays nothing; a supply pays `BACK.binPay[tier]`, shared like any income (`earn`), counted in `ws.earned` and its owner's `earned`, not in `state.sales`.
- **Machines** (Spite Still `still`, Cola Cannery `cannery`, Cord Spooler `spooler`, Tape Winder `winder`, Barb Bender `bender`): `{k, o, d, n, t, out}`. Each takes junk from any side but its front, holds up to 2, and every `BACK.makeSecs` turns one into its supply at tier 1, put on the tile in front.
- **Rebrander** `{k:'rebrand', o, d, q:[codes], t, out}`: takes any supply below tier 4 (up to 2 waiting), and puts out the same supply one tier higher after `BACK.rebrandSecs[tier]`, which is longer for each tier.

### Dumbwaiters and stock
- Every tile of a security building has a **Dumbwaiter** under it: `{k:'lift', a}` in `state.back`, `a` being the building's anchor tile. It appears and goes with the building. A security building cannot be placed where The Back already holds a part (`canPlace` answers `'back'`). Crate Stacks and Wet Floor Signs have none.
- A dumbwaiter wants a supply if its building has that track (`BUILD[k].up`, and every building with a dumbwaiter has `wire`) and the stock of that track is below its cap.
- Stock is on the building: `b.s = { dmg: [tier, uses, tier, uses, ...], ... }`, runs in the order they arrived. A unit arriving adds `usesPer(kind, track, tier)` uses to the last run if the tier matches, else starts a run. The cap is `BACK.stockUnits` units' worth.
- A track's level is `BACK.tierLevel[tier of its first run]` (`[0, 2, 4, 6, 8]`), 0 with no stock. `levelIn` in `upgrades.js` reads it, so `statsOf` and its formulas are unchanged, and an unstocked building is exactly the 0.3 building with no upgrades. Bought upgrades (`b.u`, the `upgrade` command, `levelCost`) are removed.
- Use: each shot (or each shopper a stand, sign or salesman acts on) takes one use from `dmg`, `spd` and `rng` where stocked. `BUILD[k].shots` is how many uses one unit gives that kind, so a slow gun and a fast one eat at a similar rate. Each gym bro hit takes one use of `dur` instead of counting as a hit; the tier sets how many uses a unit of Duct Tape gives (`BACK.tapeHits`).
- Shoplifters and Card Traders steal stock from the buildings they pass, not goods from belts.

### Razor Wire and dads
- Dads walk through every building; only walls and live wire stop them. `HOP` becomes `DAD`.
- A building may have a wire direction `b.wd` (0 to 3), set or cleared at any time with the `wire` command. Its wire covers the building's own tiles and the `BACK.wireReach` (3) tiles beyond it that way, stopping at a wall. The wire is **live** while the building has `wire` stock and **planned** otherwise.
- Two dad maps: `DAD` (walls and live wire block) and `DADPLAN` (walls and every wire, live or planned). The `wire` command, and placing a building that carries a wire plan, is refused (`'blocked'`) if on `DADPLAN` any entrance loses its way to the tills or a bank of tills is cut off. Live wire is always a part of planned wire, so the live map can never be cut off. `gridVer` goes up when a wire goes live or dead.
- Use: a dad is turned away by a wire when the step he would take on the walls-only map (`GHOST`) is a tile under that building's live wire. He is counted once per wire building (`s.rw` remembers the last). `BACK.wireTurns[tier]` dads use up one unit.
- Both dad routes are drawn: the live one solid, the planned one faint where it differs.

### Money
- Income: the event bonus, a speed bonus (each event has a par time; each second under par pays, up to half the event's pay), boss bounties, and the Clearance Bin. Goods and the Shipping Chute are gone, with the Unpacker, Press, Packer, the old `belt` and the marked floors (`state.zones`).
- Starting an event early no longer runs the factory ahead (`skipBuild`), so the pause is refill time and starting early gives it up.

### Sync
- The Back is part of the state, so it is sent, saved and replayed like the rest. The checksum (`session.js`) covers it in one pass: `backVer`, the number of items in The Back (hands included), the sums of their positions and codes weighted by a number from their tile, the Splitters' `h` and `r`, the Interns' `h` and `t`, the drops' `t` and `lv`, and (from stage 3) every building's stock.
- A command's values come from a player's browser: any used to look something up by name (a kind, a track, a design, a size, a difficulty, a player) is checked with `Object.hasOwn` or a list's `includes` (`buildDef(k)` for kinds), never a bare `obj[x]`.
- `stepBack` walks a cached list of parts, not the whole grid, and makes no garbage per tick: the largest store is 121 x 67 tiles and every client simulates at up to 5x speed.
