# No Receipt, No Mercy: how the code is organised

Read this before changing anything. It is the contract between the parts of the game.

## The game in one paragraph
Shoppers enter a store through its entrances (one per player, mostly on the left) and walk to the tills on the right (the Car Park has a second bank of tills in the bottom wall). Every shopper who reaches a till takes a refund, split evenly between the players; 20 refunds (5 more per extra player) and the store closes. Players build two things. On the shop floor, security (towers and stands that wear down shoppers' patience until they storm out) and Crate Stacks (cheap walls): they block shoppers, so **security and crates are the maze**. On the floor underneath, **The Back** (0.4), a factory of belts and machines turns returned junk into supplies that make the security above stronger (see "The Back" below; until 0.4 the factory stood on the shop floor, made goods and sold them, and was the maze). There are 20 waves ("sales events") with a build pause between them. Each player has his own cash and pays for what he builds. Beating a sales event pays every store player the whole event pay, plus a speed bonus for clearing the floor quickly (0.4, see "Event pay and the speed bonus"); other income and every refund are split evenly. Security buildings get stronger along separate tracks (damage, speed, range, durability) while they are stocked with supplies from The Back (0.4: bought upgrades are gone, see "The Back" below), every shopper kind has a tougher "super" version in later waves, and the store comes in several designs and sizes.

Dads are the special case the owner asked for. They arrive in a single-file "dad line", walk fast and ignore free samples. They walk by a map of their own (`DAD`): since 0.4 they walk straight through every building, and only the store's walls and live Razor Wire stop them (see "Razor Wire and dads" below; until 0.4 they only stepped over the old factory's belts). Their patience drains every second they are in the store, so the way to beat a dad is to waste his time: Razor Wire to make his walk long, wet floor signs (he ignores the sign and may slip) and towers along his route. A dad who gets through leaves a smug five-star review.

## Layers

```
index.html, src/main.js      page, menu, wiring                      (UI)
src/ui/                      HUD, build bar, speech bubbles, review  (UI, plain DOM)
src/render/                  draws the warehouse on a canvas         (rendering)
src/audio/                   sound effects, music, DEB's voice       (all synthesized, no files)
src/tutorial/lessons.js      the training shift's lessons            (no DOM; a test plays them)
src/net/session.js           HostSession / ClientSession             (multiplayer)
src/net/transports.js        how two browsers exchange messages
src/content/                 every joke, and the director that picks them
src/sim/                     the rules; no DOM, no network, no clock
```

Dependencies only point downward. `src/sim` imports nothing from the other folders and must stay runnable in plain Node (the tests do exactly that).

## Simulation (`src/sim`)
- Import from `src/sim/index.js` only.
- The game state is one plain JSON-safe object made by `createGame()`. Its fields are documented in `src/sim/state.js`.
- `step(state)` advances one tick (30 ticks a second) and returns the events of that tick.
- `applyCommand(state, playerId, cmd)` is the only way players change the state. It validates everything.
- `canPlace(state, kind, x, y, playerId, facing)` returns `null` if that player may put a building there, otherwise a reason string (`'fit'` when a building bigger than one tile does not fit). The UI uses it to colour the placement preview.
- It is deterministic: random numbers come from `rand(state)`, never `Math.random()`. Keep it that way; clients rely on it to predict the game between host updates.
- All tuning numbers and all definitions of buildings and shoppers are in `src/sim/constants.js`.

Maps: `src/sim/maps.js` builds the floor plan from a design and a size (`createGame({ map: { design, size } })`; left out, it is the original 30 x 16 warehouse with one entrance, which the tutorial and most tests use). The plan lives in the state: `state.w`, `state.h`, `state.tiles` (one string; the characters are explained in `maps.js`), `state.doors` (the entrances in the order they open) and `state.open` (how many are open: one per player). Entrances that are still shut exist from the start and count as floor for path-finding, so the maze can never wall one in. Nothing may assume a fixed map size.

Furniture: the Food Court design fills the floor with tables, benches, planters, bins, food stalls and a fountain. Each is an ordinary `'#'` in `state.tiles`, so every rule treats it as solid; `state.decor` is a second string, one character per tile, that says what each solid tile looks like (`DECOR` in `maps.js` names the characters). It is `''` for the designs without furniture and missing in old saves. Only the drawing code reads it (and the director, to leave out jokes about a fountain or a burger van the map does not have).

The Car Park: its parked cars work the same way (`park()` in `maps.js`; the letters are in `DECOR` too). Rows of parking bays run up and down the strips between where the old factory's marked floor was (see "Where the old marked floor was" below), so the lanes between the rows are open floor. A row of cars is a wall, with ways through: one straight across every row at the height of the first entrance (the walkway to the right-hand tills) and others staggered from row to row. It is the only design with two banks of tills. `tillBanks(state)` in `path.js` lists the banks of any store (each run of `'T'` tiles along a wall); shoppers walk to the nearest till whichever bank it is in, so nothing else in the rules needed to know. `canPlace` refuses, with the reason `'bank'`, a building that would cut any bank off from the others (`banksOpen`), so a second bank cannot simply be walled up. The Car Park also opens its entrances in a different order: the two in the bottom wall last. Nothing may assume the tills are in the right-hand wall, or that there is one bank.

The Back Passage (key `corridors`, 0.3): long walls run across the store and cut it into corridors (four on the smallest size, five on the next two, six on the three-player size, up to fifteen on the biggest), leaving a hall three tiles wide along the left wall and the right one; the walls have few openings (in every other gap between two sections of the old marked floor, taking turns from wall to wall). `passagePlan(w, h)` in `maps.js` works the layout out from the width and height alone, and both the walls (`passages`) and the old marked floor's patches (`passagePatches`, see "Where the old marked floor was") read it, so they always agree. (Until 0.4 each section's four kinds of marked floor were in different corridors, so a production line had to go through the openings; the walls go all the way down, so The Back's belts do too.) Patches are two rows tall, one on the Corner Shop (two would leave room for only three corridors there; a store with only three, which no size has any more, puts the Press Shop and Gift Wrap together in the middle one). Its plot is taller than the size's rectangle and as wide (0.3.01): `passageSize(size)` takes the lowest height that gives the size at least its least number of corridors (`PASSAGE_LEAST`) and at least an Open Warehouse's floor, preferring one where the top and bottom corridors have a row to spare, so no Loading Bay loses a tile to the floor in front of an entrance (only the Outlet, 36 x 26, still loses one: the next height that avoids it has 30% more floor than the warehouse). The floor comes out 0 to 8% bigger than the warehouse's, 18% on the Outlet, where five corridors need that height. A game keeps the plan it was made with (`state.tiles`, `w`, `h`). The walls are ordinary `'#'` tiles (no decor); the renderer draws them as breeze block when the design is `corridors`.

The Strip Mall (key `horseshoe`, 0.3): the store is a U round a car park that is not part of the store. `lotPlan(w, h)` in `maps.js` cuts a block up from the middle of the bottom wall (about a third of the width, from 45% of the way down); the block's left, right and top edges are ordinary `'#'` store wall, and everything inside them, down to the bottom edge of the map, is `'#'` in `state.tiles` with `'O'` in `state.decor` (`DECOR.O`). So every rule (walking, building, path maps, the checks that keep the tills reachable) already treats it as solid, and only the drawing changes: the renderer draws `'O'` tiles as an outdoor car park (`drawLot` in `floor.js`: tarmac, bays and parked cars, a pavement with shop awnings and signs, a sign on a pole) and the walls round it as outside walls; the minimap draws it dark. The Strip Mall has its own entrances (`stripDoors`: all seven on the left arm, three in the left wall and two each in the top and bottom walls of the arm) and its tills low on the right arm (`stripTills`), so everyone walks round the bend; every entrance is 1.0 to 1.2 times as far from the tills as the first one. Its old marked floor (`stripPatches`, which places its Returns Drops) has sections placed to fit the U: centred in each arm over its whole height, and over the car park in the bend, as tall as the bend allows (thinner patches on the two smallest sizes). Its plot is bigger than the size's rectangle: `stripSize(size)` scales the rectangle until the floor inside the U is as near as it gets to an Open Warehouse's of that size (within 3% on every size).

Map sizes: `mapSize(design, size)` gives a store's width and height: the rectangle in `MAP_SIZES`, except the Back Passage's taller plot and the Strip Mall's bigger one. Code outside `maps.js` must read the made map's `state.w` and `state.h`, never `MAP_SIZES[size].w` (the menu's and lobby's size pickers label each size with `labelSizes(select, design)` in `src/ui/settings.js`). `factoryPatches(w, h, tiles, doors, design)` and `makeDrops` take the design as a fifth argument: designs with a layout of their own (the Back Passage, the Strip Mall) lay the old marked floor their own way (without it, the ordinary layout).

Where the old marked floor was: until 0.4 the shop floor had patches of painted floor where the old factory's machines had to go (`state.zones`, `ZONES`, the `'zone'` refusal). They went with that factory in 0.4, but `factoryPatches()` in `maps.js` still works out where they were, for two things only, so that every store stays exactly as it was: the Returns Drops stand on the old Loading Bay patches (`makeDrops`), and the food court's and car park's furniture keeps clear of every patch (`reserve`). `test/back.test.js` pins every drop, and a hash of the tiles and furniture, on every design and size.

Players who drop out: a player who goes offline keeps his money for `TUNE.graceSecs` (90 seconds of real time: `player.gone` counts how long he has been away in ticks at normal speed, so each tick at 3x speed counts for a third, at 5x a fifth). If he is not back by then, `step()` shares it out between the players still connected, and he returns with none. A host who leaves gets the same grace. In the lobby nothing changes hands.

Pausing (0.3.02): `state.pause` is `{ by }` (the id of the player who paused) while the game is paused, and absent otherwise (as in every save from before it, and in every game the scripted players play). The command is `{ c: 'pause', on }` (`on` true pauses, false resumes; left out, it flips): any connected player may send it, Head Office too, in the phases `'cards'`, `'build'` and `'wave'` (in the lobby it is refused with `'lobby'`, once the game is won or lost with `'over'`); pausing a paused game, or resuming one that is not, is a yes that changes nothing. When it changes, it sends the event `pause {on, by}`. While paused, `step()` returns at once, before even `state.tick` moves, exactly as in the lobby: no shoppers, belts, timers, build countdown, card deal or grace periods for players who left; so a game paused for any number of steps and then resumed carries on exactly as one never paused. `applyCommand` refuses everything with `'paused'` except `pause`, `speed`, `role` and `skipCards` (the deal waits all the same); a rebuild paid for before the pause waits through it and goes back once the game carries on; `canPlace` gives `'paused'` too, so the preview turns red. The reason nothing can be built: the build pause between sales events runs on a timer that stands still while paused, and building in a pause would make that timer, and the pressure of a sales event, meaningless. Players joining, leaving and coming back are not commands (`addPlayer`, `setPlayerOnline`) and work as ever; the state, pause included, goes to a joining player and into a save, so both land paused.

Money: `state.players[i].cash`. There is no shared pot. `earn()` and `lose()` in `state.js` split income and refunds evenly between the connected players; `teamCash(state)` adds everyone's up for display. The event pay and its speed bonus are not split: `endWave` pays each player sharing income the whole of both (`earn(state, amount * shareCount(state))`).

Event pay and the speed bonus (0.4, removal step 2; the owner's decision: the factory no longer makes money, beating a sales event does). `eventPay(state, n)` in `step.js` is what sales event n pays each store player: `round((TUNE.payBase + TUNE.payStep * n) * difficulty pay * cardPay(state))`, 150 + 45 an event on Normal (it was 88 + 16.5 while goods paid the bills). The speed bonus is `speedBonus(state, n, pay, to, from)`, the one function `endWave` pays with and the EVENT PAY panel shows: its clock starts at `state.lastIn`, the tick the event's last shopper came in through a door (set by `spawn()` when it empties `state.spawnQ`, or by `beginWave` for an event nobody comes to; absent between events and while shoppers are still to come), and stops when the floor is clear (the tick `endWave` runs). `took` is those ticks in seconds, so game speed makes no difference. `share` is 1 at `TUNE.speedBest` (10) seconds or less, 0 at `speedPar(n)` or more (`TUNE.speedPar`, 60; twice that in a boss event), a straight line between. `clean` is `ws.stopped / (ws.stopped + ws.escaped)` for the event (1 when both are 0): the shoppers who stormed out over those plus those who took a refund toward the limit. Babies and puppies are in neither count (as in the review: a popped lady's dozen would swamp it); the boss counts once either way, like anyone; a mom's runaway child is not a shopper of his own. The bonus is `round(pay * TUNE.speedShare * share * clean)` (`speedShare` 0.5); before the clock starts `speedBonus` gives its most, `max`. `endWave` (not in training) pays both, records `ws.pay`, `ws.speed` and `ws.took`, deletes `lastIn`, and sends `waveEnd {wave, pay, speed, took}` (a training wave sends 0s). The checksum mixes in `lastIn` (while there is one) and the event's `stopped` and `escaped`. `migrateSave` gives a save made mid-event after the last shopper came in a `lastIn` of now, and drops the old `sales` total.

EVENT PAY on screen (0.4, in the place of the INCOME panel of 0.3.03, whose `src/ui/income.js` and `rate.js` are gone): `src/ui/eventpay.js`. `payView(state)` (pure, tested in Node) gives what it shows: the current event's pay (between events, the next one's, marked NEXT), and the speed bonus, "up to $X" until the last shopper is in, then the live figure from `speedBonus` (already scaled by `clean` so far), green while it is whole, amber as it drains, struck through once it is gone; the clock (seconds since the last shopper came in) is in the panel's title, and a small line under it says how many are still to come, or the par and the share stopped. With several store players it says "each, all 3 of you" (Head Office sees "each store player"). Shown in the build phase and sales events only (not the lobby, the deal, training, or once the game is won or lost). On a big screen it is the third panel in the left column (ON SHIFT, HAND, EVENT PAY), placed by `placeStrip` in `game.js` exactly as the INCOME panel was; hovering or focusing it shows the rule. On a small screen it is a chip ("$465 +$233") in the top-left row (`placeChip`), tapping it opens a card. The end-of-event report (`src/ui/review.js`) shows Event pay and "Speed bonus · 0:39" as two of its figures (and Stock stolen in place of Goods stolen). The money rule in words for the handbook, the help and the README is `payText()` / `payFacts()` in `facts.js`, from the rules. DEB has `speedFull` and `speedNone` (all of it, or none at all; nothing in between), note topics said with the end of an event.

Levels: `src/sim/upgrades.js`. A security building has up to four tracks: `dmg` (damage, or the building's main effect), `spd` (fires faster), `rng` (range) and `dur` (durability). Which tracks a kind has and what a level does in each are in `BUILD[kind].up` (Crate Stacks have none). Since 0.4 the levels come only from the building's stock of supplies from The Back (`levelIn(b, track)` is `stockLevel`: see "The Back"); bought upgrades, the `upgrade` command, `canUpgrade`, the Upgrade tool and the U key are gone (0.4, the owner's decision: supplies replace them). `tracksOf(kind)` lists a kind's tracks and `levelOf(b)` is the number drawn on the badge (1 plus the level every track works at). `spentOn(b)` is what was paid for it (`c`, or the kind's price). `statsOf(b)` gives the building's numbers with its stock applied and is the only place the simulation or the interface should read damage, range, firing time or the hits it survives from. `trackName` and `trackNumbers` in `src/content/supplies.js` turn a track into words ("Damage: hit 9.4") for the building sheet, the handbook and the README.

Buildings bigger than one tile: `BUILD[kind].size` is `[width, height]` when facing east or west; facing north or south swaps them. The building lives on its top-left tile (the anchor); every other tile it covers holds `{ k: sameKind, part: anchorIndex }`. Always go through `anchorOf(state, tileIndex)` to get from any covered tile to the building, `tilesOf(state, anchorIndex)` or `footprint(kind, x, y, facing, width)` for the tiles, and `centreOf(state, anchorIndex)` for where it shoots or reaches from. The Barcode Laser (key `railgun`: it was called the Rail Gun until 0.2.06, and the key was kept so that saved games and commands still work) is 3 x 1 and fires out of its front end along the way it faces; the TV Salesman is 2 x 2.

Wrecked buildings: a gym bro's hit adds one to `b.hit`; a building is wrecked when that reaches `statsOf(b).hits` (1 unless the kind says more, plus one for each level of a reinforced Crate Stack; Duct Tape soaks up hits before they count, see "The Back"), and every `b.hit` is cleared when the wave ends. A wrecked building leaves an entry in `state.rubble` that remembers its kind, facing and owner (and `c`, `bz` when a card gave it those, `r` a crate's reinforcement, `wd` a wire's direction); its stock is lost.

Reinforced Crate Stacks (0.4, the owner's request: "I expect there to be a lot more crates used as filler where belts are needed in The Back, and fragile crates will make gym bros too overpowered"): the command `{ c: 'reinforce', x, y }` raises the crate's level `b.r` by one (absent for none), and each level is one more gym bro hit it takes in a sales event (`statsOf(b).hits` is 1 + `r`; hit counts still reset every event). `reinforceCost(b, state)` in `upgrades.js` is the next level's price: `round(cost * grow ** r)` from `BUILD.crate.reinforce` (`cost` 10, `grow` 1.6, `max` 8: 10, 16, 26, 41, 66, 105, 168, 268, the crate's durability track of 0.3.04), times the cards that change a crate's price (`priceMul(state, k)` in `cards.js`, which `buildPrice` uses too: shrinkflation, staffdiscount, rentacop, sweatshop), whole dollars, never under one; 0 at the top level. Anyone in the store may pay for it (as for a Returns Drop's level); in the build phase or a sales event, not paused. The money counts as spent on the building: it is added to `b.c`, so `spentOn` includes it, selling it back returns `TUNE.sellBack` of it to the owner, and a wrecked one is rebuilt, levels and all, for `TUNE.rebuildShare` of everything spent, all or nothing. `canReinforce(state, pid, x, y)` gives the reasons: `'over'`, `'lobby'`, `'cards'`, `'paused'`, `'bad'` (no such player), `'office'`, `'bounds'`, `'empty'`, `'kind'` (not a Crate Stack), `'max'`, `'cash'`. Event `reinforce {x, y, r, by, paid}` (the anchor tile). Counted in the payer's `spent`. `rebuildDeal(entry, cash)` works out what its owner pays to put it back: `TUNE.rebuildShare` (half) of what was paid for it, all or nothing (0.4: until then a player short of money got it back with fewer of its bought upgrades), or null if he cannot afford it. The `rebuild` command (the B key, the Rebuild buttons) orders it for all of a player's rubble (below), and placing the same kind on its own rubble uses the same deal.

Rebuilding is asked for, not automatic (0.3.04; auto-rebuild, 0.2.15 to 0.3.03, is gone: the owner found it made the game too passive). The `rebuild` command (`{ c: 'rebuild' }`, `all: true` for everybody's) is allowed in the build phase and a sales event only, not while paused (`'paused'`), not during the deal (`'cards'`) and not for Head Office (`'office'`). `orderRebuilds()` in `commands.js` goes through the sender's wrecks oldest first, leaving out ones already ordered: rubble that has been built over is tidied away (which is why the session forwards a refused `rebuild` to its clients); a wreck he cannot afford (`rebuildDeal` gives null) is not ordered (the owner's decision: he presses again when he has the money); any other is ordered: `deal.pay` leaves his cash at once and the wreck gets `by` (his id) and `res` (the whole dollars held for it). A record without `by` is not ordered (test `by !== undefined`). It then goes back at once if it can. The result is `{ ok: true, n, held, paid }` (put back now, ordered and waiting, money taken), or `{ ok: false, why }` with `'cash'` (too broke for any), `'ordered'` (everything of his already waits) or `'empty'`. Events: `rebuilt {by, n}` and `rebuildHeld {by, n, paid}` (`paid` there is only the money held for the wrecks left waiting; the result's `paid` is all the press took). There is no cancel (the owner's decision). Waiting orders: `stepRebuilds(state, ev)`, called by `step()` after the shoppers move, does nothing unless something is ordered, then every `TUNE.rebuildEvery` (0.2 s of game time; a refused check costs a path search), oldest first: an order whose payer is gone or Head Office is cancelled and repaid; an order always holds its whole rebuild price, so nothing more is ever taken while it waits (until 0.4 an order paid while short took more as cash came in), and a payer who is away still gets what he paid for; then `putBack()` builds it if every `canPlace` check but the cash passes (`placeCheck(..., paid)`, shared with `canPlace`; `canPutBack(state, r)` gives the reason to the interface), with `rebuildDeal(r, res)` exactly as an immediate rebuild (owner = the payer, card price and booze kept; `spent` and `built` counted now). One `rebuilt` event per payer with `late: 1`. Held money is never lost: `releaseOrder(state, r)` in `state.js` gives `res` back to `by` whenever an ordered wreck stops being rubble any other way (built over, `clearRubble`; off the end of the 400-long list in `wreck()`; its payer taking Head Office; the game won or lost, `releaseAllOrders`, because the end screen counts cash). When an away player's grace period runs out, `expireAway` cancels his orders first, so the held money is shared out with the rest of his cash and no later refund can go to someone who has left; a player back within his grace period keeps his orders. Placing the same kind on a wreck he has paid for counts `res` as his cash in `canPlace`, and the `place` command repays any order on that wreck before charging, so he pays once. `heldFor(state, pid)` adds up a player's held money; the checksum mixes the total in. On screen: `createRebuildPop()` in `src/ui/rebuild.js` floats a big REBUILD button over the bottom middle of the floor (clear of the entrances, the tills, DEB's bubble, the camera pad and the small map; game.js lifts it onto the held tool's card) while the player has a wreck, ordered or not. `rebuildStatus(state, me)` decides it without changing anything: wrecks not ordered (`n`, `cost`), whether B would order one (`can`, `why`), and those he has paid for (`held`, `paid`, `full`, `wait`: why the oldest waits, one path search); `rebuildWords` turns that into the three looks: lit (REBUILD 3 · $450), dimmed with the reason (Too broke. Need $X), and waiting (PAID $300 · 2 waiting, no nudge). The build bar's Rebuild button has the same idle / alert / waiting states, and the renderer puts a little orange cone on an ordered wreck (`drawRubble(..., ordered)`).

Opening the doors early (0.4): the `start` command just calls `beginWave()`. Until 0.4 `skipBuild()` played the rest of the build timer through in one go and paid for it (the `early` event, DEB's `early` lines and a notice); that is gone with the factory's income: what The Back would have made in the time skipped, and the drying of puddles, is given up.

Security switched off: `b.off` is the number of ticks a building stays out of action after a stray dog pees on it. `step()` counts it down; a building with `b.off` does not fire, stop or pitch.

Shoppers that affect other shoppers: a Day Drinker lying on a tile slows everyone crossing it; a Cosplayer takes the shots aimed at anyone within `TUNE.shield` tiles of him; an Exhibitionist stops shoppers whose definition has `gawks` (dads and teenagers); the TV Salesman rolls once per shopper using `SHOPPERS[kind].pitch = [chance to stop, chance to hurry]`. A shopper who stops first walks over to the stand (`s.sa` is the stand's tile while he does; `walkToStand()` in `step.js`), as near as he can get, and only then goes into `ST.WATCH`. A mom's child may run away once (twice for the Soccer Mom), never nearer than `TUNE.kidTill` tiles of walking from a till, and she will not take her refund without him; after a mom has gone down she has no cart and carries the child.

The boss: every fifth wave (`TUNE.bossEvery`) one boss walks in, as the last group `waveSpec()` returns. His definition is `BOSS` in `constants.js`, deliberately NOT in `SHOPPERS` or `SUPERS`, so nothing that lists the kinds of shopper picks him up by accident; `defOf(shopper)` and `shopperDef('boss')` return it. A boss shopper has `k: 'boss'`, `tr` (his tier: the wave divided by five, which also picks his title with `bossTitle(tier)`) and `pp` (ticks to his next pep talk, which gives shoppers near him some patience back). Nothing slows or stops him. Reaching a till he counts as `TUNE.bossRefunds` refunds (2 of a limit of 20; it was 5 of 40); worn out, he pays the store a bounty. He is not scaled by the patience multiplier (`patienceScaleAt`), and his refund is not multiplied by `perHead()`; his event's crowd is halved by `TUNE.crowdScale` like any other, on top of `TUNE.bossCrowd`. The other groups of a boss wave are `TUNE.bossCrowd` of their usual size. Events: `bossIn`, `pep`, `bossOut`, and `escape` with `n` (the refunds it counted). Head Office cannot buy him and he comes whatever was planned.

Holidays (0.3): `state.holiday` is a key of `HOLIDAYS` in `constants.js` (`xmas`, `thanksgiving`, `valentine`, `july4`, `halloween`, `stpat`, `easter`, `newyear`, `mardigras`), or absent for none (as in every save from before it, and every test game). `createGame({ holiday })` keeps a key on that list and drops anything else. No rule reads it: it only dresses the bosses up and gives DEB holiday lines, and a game plays out exactly the same with or without it. The simulation has no clock, so the calendar is outside it, in `src/content/holidays.js`: `holidayOn(date)` gives the holiday whose window (the 7 days before it, the day and the day after, by the date's own local calendar) contains that date, or null; where two windows overlap (Christmas and New Year's, Valentine's and Mardi Gras), the holiday whose day is nearer wins, and on a tie the one still to come. Easter is worked out for any year (Mardi Gras is 47 days before it), Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday of November. Only the host's start-up code asks it: `main.js` passes `holidayFor(urlValue)` (today's on the host's computer, or the `&holiday=` test option) to `new HostSession({ holiday })` and to `backToLobby({ holiday })`, so a new game, and a game restarted from the end screen, gets the holiday of the day it was made. Everyone else gets it with the state: a joined player sees the same outfit and hears the same lines, and a saved game keeps its own holiday when resumed. `HOLIDAY_INFO` there names each holiday and what the boss wears (`outfitText(key)`, "dressed as Santa", which the boss bar puts after his title and the handbook's boss card under "Dress code").

The result is multiplied by `TUNE.crowdTrim[n]` where an event has one (event 5: 0.93). The endless cap `TUNE.maxCrowd` takes the same share. Only the head count moves; patience, refund cash and prices do not follow it. The handbook and README take their counts from `waveSpec()`, so they follow. A further round of "so many per cent fewer than now" is one more curve in the list.

Fewer, tougher shoppers (0.2.09): numbers in `TUNE` scale the flood, and the owner expects to push them further. `TUNE.crowdScale` (0.5) multiplies every group `waveSpec()` makes (to the nearest whole number, a half rounded down, never below one, so every kind is still there; a group arrives over the same length of time, so its gaps grow) and the cap `TUNE.maxCrowd`, from the first sales event. Shoppers get tougher to make up for it, but only as the game goes on: `patienceScaleAt(n)` in `step.js` is 1 up to and including event `TUNE.patienceFrom` (5), rises in a straight line to `TUNE.patienceScale` (2) at event `TUNE.patienceFull` (20), and stays there. It multiplies the patience every shopper walks in with (`patienceFor(state, def)`, the one place it is worked out; difficulty, growth by wave and a super's multiplier apply on top; Head Office's shoppers and the Pregnant Lady's babies too) and a dad's drain. Why phased: while security has time to spare between arrivals (the early events), a shopper's fate depends only on his own patience against what one walk past the towers does to him, so at x1 half the crowd leaks the same share as before, which is neutral against a refund limit also halved. Once security is busy all the time (the late events), what matters is total patience against total damage, and there half the crowd at x2 is neutral. A flat x2 from the first event made the start far harder (PLAN.md, version 0.2.09, has the numbers). Neither applies to the boss (one man, not the flood) or in a training game (the lessons queue their own waves and are played as written). What is counted per shopper follows the crowd, so the economy keeps its proportions: `perHead()` in `constants.js` (1 / crowdScale) multiplies the cash a refund takes, Head Office's prices (`priceOf`), and `OFFICE.perRefund` and every `OFFICE.kinds[k].gap` (these two once, when the game loads). Damage from buildings, slips and anything that is a share of a shopper's own patience (pep talks, a mom getting up) are not scaled. `src/content/facts.js` works out the numbers the same way (`patienceAt`, `toughnessAt`), so the handbook and README match the game event by event.

The Pregnant Lady (`preggo`; super: Octomom) is an ordinary kind, with `pops` (12, or 24) in her definition. When her patience runs out, `hurt()` in `step.js` looks at what took the last of it (`from`, the building's kind): a Barcode Laser (`railgun`) and she storms off like anyone, with a `nopop` event after her `left`; anything else and she pops. A popped lady sends `left` with `why: 'pop'`, then `pop`; she is removed on the shoppers' next move (the same tick, as buildings always do it) and counts as stopped, and `pops` babies are added to `state.shoppers` round where she stood (within `TUNE.popSpread`, on her own tile). Babies follow the boss's pattern: their definition is `BABY` in `constants.js`, deliberately not in `SHOPPERS` or `SUPERS`, so nothing that lists kinds picks them up and Head Office cannot buy them; a baby is `{ k: 'baby' }` with only the ordinary shopper fields (never `sup`), and `defOf()` and `shopperDef('baby')` return `BABY`. If she is finished while standing on a till (buildings act before shoppers move, so it can happen the tick after she steps onto one), nothing pops: she storms off without her refund, as any shopper finished on a till does. They walk the ordinary route (`modeOf` gives `NORMAL`), very fast (each at its own pace, `babyPace(id)`: between `BABY.paceMin` and `BABY.paceMax` of `BABY.speed`, worked out from its id, so a litter strings out without any field in the state or random draw), with very little patience (grown and scaled like anyone's), are slowed by wet floors, messes and a fallen drunk, and ignore free samples, stock to steal, the TV Salesman and an exhibitionist. A baby at a till takes `BABY.refund` ($5, not scaled) and is no refund toward the limit: its `escape` event has `n: 0`, and it is not counted in `state.ws.escaped`. A baby who runs out of patience just leaves (`left`, `why: 'annoyed'`) and is not counted in `ws.stopped` or a player's `stopped`. A sales event is not over while babies are in the store. The director gives babies no reviews and leaves them out of a run of refunds; a popped lady gets DEB's `pop` line instead of a review.

Host hand-over: `state.host` is the player whose browser runs the game. When a joined player's connection to the host closes, `hostLost()` in `src/main.js` runs in every remaining browser. All of them pick the same successor (the first connected player in the list who is not the old host). The successor makes a `HostSession` from its own copy of the state with `takeOver: true` and opens the room again under the same code, retrying until the old host's code is released; the others retry joining it. Players are matched to their old places by name. The game screen is torn down and started again on the new session (`destroy()` on what `startGame` returns).

Saving: `src/ui/save.js` keeps one save in the host browser's `localStorage` (`keepSaved(session)` writes the whole state every few seconds, at each wave's end and when the page closes; `readSave()` gives it back with a summary for the menu, or null if there is none or it does not look right). `actions.resume()` in `src/main.js` makes a `HostSession` from it with `resume: true`, which seats the host by name and treats everyone else as away, money intact, until he rejoins. The state carries its version (`state.v`, `STATE_VERSION` in `state.js`, 5 since 0.4). 0.4 loads no older save (the owner's decision: the factory moved under the shop), so `readSave` accepts only this version and there is no migration; the menu calls `dropOldSave()`, which throws out a save of any other version and returns the version of the game that made it, so the player is told once, in the menu's status line, why his saved game is not offered. Only real games are saved: not training, not the lobby, not finished games, not the URL test options. (A game that was won and then carried on as endless is a game in progress again, and is saved from the moment of the `endless` event.) `src/ui/record.js` keeps, per difficulty, the best sales event reached in an endless game in the same browser storage; the lost screen and the menu show it.

Employee Handbook and README lists: `src/content/facts.js` works out, from the rules, the numbers people are shown: a shopper's patience in any wave, how many turn up, what its super version changes, and, for every security building, a row per tier of each supply it takes, with what that tier makes it work at (`tierRows(kind, track)`, from `statsOf` with a stock). `src/ui/handbook.js` is the in-game handbook (three tabs: Known offenders, Shop floor (the Crate Stack and security), The Back (its parts, the five supplies, the brand tiers); opened from the menu, the book button in the top bar and the ? screen; in a game it uses that game's difficulty and player count). Its tables by sales event go up to the longest game the length drop-down offers (`LONGEST` in `facts.js`, the biggest of `SETTINGS.waves`), whatever the game's own length; an endless game's go further (`sampleWaves`, `bossWaves`). `src/ui/portrait.js` draws a shopper with the renderer's own code (`drawShopper` in `src/render/shoppers.js`; its last argument says what a watching shopper is looking at, and `noBar` leaves the patience bar out). `tools/readme.mjs` writes the version, the store designs and the shoppers into `README.md` between `<!-- gen:... -->` markers and, with `--shots`, retakes the pictures in `docs/img/` (maps from `tools/render-harness.html`, shoppers from `tools/portrait.html`). `test/readme.test.js` fails if the README is out of date and checks the handbook's numbers against the simulation.

Touch wording: lessons and help are written once, for mouse and keyboard. `src/content/touch.js` rewords them for touch screens (`worded(text)`), and a test fails if any lesson or help text still names a key or the mouse afterwards. Add a rule there when a new lesson mentions a key.

Installing: `manifest.webmanifest`, the icons in `icons/` (photographs of `tools/icon.html`) and `sw.js` let phones offer "Install app". `sw.js` deliberately caches nothing, so the newest version always loads.

Boss bar: `src/ui/bossbar.js` is the strip over the top of the floor that announces the next boss during the build phase and shows his title and patience while he is in the store. It does the same, in purple, for a Midnight Release: the head count before it, a countdown to the mob, then how many of the mob are left. It floats over the floor and has a fixed height, so it never resizes anything.

Chat: `session.chat(text)` sends a line to everyone and `session.on('chat', fn)` receives `{ pid, name, color, text }`. The host tidies and rate-limits it; it is not part of the game state. `src/ui/chat.js` is the box.

Touch: `src/ui/input.js` treats `pointerType` 'touch' and 'pen' (a pen since 0.3.03) separately from the mouse. Two fingers always pan and pinch-zoom and never build; one finger with nothing held drags the view, and a tap selects a building. Since 0.3.01 a finger places one thing at a time: with any building but the belt held, or the Remove tool, the preview (`view.hover`) follows the finger while it is down, with dashed lines along the tile's row and column (`view.aim`) because the finger hides the tile, and the building is placed (removed, or its sheet opened) once, on the tile the finger lifts from; lifted off the map or over any interface element (`elementFromPoint` is not the canvas) nothing happens. Only the belt still lays a line when dragged (tiles are laid as the finger moves). A second finger on the canvas while the first is placing cancels it: nothing more is built when either lifts (belt tiles a drag has already laid stay; the one under the finger is not laid), the held building stays selected, and the two fingers pan and pinch. `input.placing` gives the placing finger's position, or null. Since 0.3.03 (the owner's requests) a building's preview is lifted above the finger placing it, and it goes where the preview is: once the finger has moved `TAP_SLOP_PX` (8) from where it went down (`createPress`; time plays no part, and once up it stays up for that touch), the footprint is centred `liftPx(zoom)` above it (1.5 tiles, clamped to 56-110 CSS px, eased in over 140 ms; `liftedSpot`, `liftedAnchor`, which keeps the footprint inside the map); a finger that never moves that far places on the tapped tile. The lifted point must be on the floor itself (`elementFromPoint`), or nothing is placed. While lifted, `view.aim` is false and `view.lift` gives the finger in tiles, from which the renderer draws a thin line up to the preview (`drawLift`); the Remove tool, the belt and a resting finger keep `view.aim`. A building can also be dragged out of the bar: `input.watchBar(el, { onPick })` (called by `buildbar.js` on the bar's scroller) listens for a finger or pen pressed on a button with `data-k` of a building, and, on the window in the capture phase, for its moves: `barGesture(dx, dy, outOfBar)` decides 'pick' (12 px mostly up, or onto the floor), 'scroll' (mostly sideways: the bar has `touch-action: pan-x`, so the browser scrolls it and cancels the pointer) or 'wait'. A picked building (`input.carrying`, `input.placingKind`; `view.tool` is it meanwhile, `input.tool` never changes) is placed once where its lifted preview is when the finger lifts; a `pointercancel`, a finger landing on the canvas, or losing focus ends it with nothing placed; the click the button may get afterwards is swallowed. In `input.tick`, a lifted placement within `EDGE.bandPx` (48) of an edge of the canvas pans the view by `edgeVelocity` (up to 8 tiles a second, deeper is faster; for a bar drag the bottom edge counts only once the finger has been above that band) and the preview's tile is recomputed. The numbers and decisions are in `src/ui/lift.js`, pure and tested in Node. `src/ui/touch.js` adds the on-screen buttons for what the keyboard does, the sheet of actions for a tapped building, and, while a finger is placing, the placing aid: a 64-pixel Rotate button (only for a building that turns) and the hint "Tap the floor with another finger to cancel", halfway up the side of the floor away from the finger. Its Rotate button, like the one in the bar, acts on `pointerdown`, because a second finger's tap may never become a click; the hint has `pointer-events: none`, so a finger on it lands on the floor. All of these appear only on `(pointer: coarse)` devices. Phone and tablet layouts are one block at the end of `src/style.css`, switched on by screen size; desktop layouts must not change.

Settings: `SETTINGS` in `constants.js` lists what a host may choose: the number of waves, each player's starting cash, and a difficulty (which multiplies shopper patience, including the boss's and the babies', and income: the event pay (and so its speed bonus), a stand's sales (the warranty card) and the boss's bounty). Normal is the baseline, x1 and x1 by definition, so every number in `constants.js` is Normal's and the handbook and README show them as they are; the other levels are round numbers relative to it (Easy x0.75 patience and x1.25 income, Hard x1.3 and x0.85, Brutal x1.65 and x0.7). Money is rounded to whole dollars after the multiplier, so a base price can be a decimal (a Gadget's 7.7 pays $8 on Normal); anything shown to a player must be the rounded amount. A training game uses no difficulty: its shoppers get base patience divided by `TUNE.trainingPatienceDivisor` (0.9), the numbers its lessons were written for before 0.2.10 made Normal the baseline. They live in the state (`state.waves`, `state.startCash`, `state.difficulty`), are given to `createGame({ settings })`, and can be changed in the lobby with `{ c: 'settings', ... }`. Read them from the state, never from `TUNE`. `src/ui/settings.js` is the set of drop-downs the menu and the lobby share (since 0.3.01 also Cards: On / Off, see the cards above; `cleanSettings` gives `cardsOn`, true unless it is false).

Lobby: a game made with `createGame({ lobby: true })` starts in phase `'lobby'`. Nothing runs and nothing can be built; the host (player 0) may send `{ c: 'map', design, size }`, which swaps the store with `setMap()` and clears everyone's `ready`; each player sends `{ c: 'ready', on }`; `step()` moves to `'build'` when every connected player is ready (to `'cards'`, the deal, first in a game with cards still to deal; see the cards above). Games started from the menu use it; the URL test options do not unless `&lobby=1` is given. `src/ui/lobby.js` is the panel.

Head Office: `src/sim/office.js`. One player may switch `side` to `'office'` and buy the coming wave from a budget instead of building (`state.office`, the `side` and `plan` commands). His plan is turned into the same list of groups `waveSpec()` produces, with an entrance per group. He is left out of everything that counts store players (`shareCount`, income, the review).

Path maps: there are five (`NORMAL`; `DAD` for dads (0.4: only the store's walls and live Razor Wire block it, `getDadDist`); `SMASH` for gym bros; `GHOST` for runaway children, who are stopped only by the store's walls; and `DADPLAN`, which nobody walks: the dads' map as it would be with every planned wire stocked, walls and all wire blocking, `getDadPlanDist`, which the `wire` command is checked on and the interface draws), plus `getFieldTo(state, tile)` for a mom running to where her child waits. On the two dad maps a till under wire is no way out. All are cached until `state.gridVer` changes, and so is where the wire is (`wireMap`), so everything that changes the wire moves `gridVer` on (see "Razor Wire and dads"). `modeOf(kind)` is a kind's own map; `routeOf(shopper)` is the one he walks by right now (a gym bro who cannot hit anything at the moment walks `NORMAL`, see below). `canPlace` checks entrances, banks of tills and every shopper in the store on `NORMAL` only: dads walk through buildings, so a building can never wall a dad in, and one standing on a tile does not stop a building going there (anyone else does: `'shopper'`).

Gym bros' limit (0.2.12, the owner's numbers): a gym bro lands at most `smashMax` hits in one visit (2; the Roid Rager 3) and rests `smashWait` seconds after each (10; the Roid Rager 5); both are in `TUNE` with the Roid Rager's own in `SHOPPERS.gymbro.sup`, read with `tv(def, key)`. Every hit he lands counts, even one Duct Tape soaks up. The shopper fields are `hn` (hits landed) and `hw` (ticks of rest left, counted down whatever he is doing); a missing field reads as 0. `smashReady(shopper)` in `path.js` says whether he may hit now; while he may not (resting, or spent), he hits nothing and walks the `NORMAL` map like anyone else, so he never stands at a crate waiting. `smashEvery` is still the time between attempts that did not land. Standing on a crate tile still flattens it at once, without using a hit. Because he may become an ordinary walker at any moment, `canPlace` checks gym bros on the `NORMAL` map: nobody may wall one in with crates.

Coordinates: the grid is `state.w` x `state.h` tiles. A tile index is `y * state.w + x`. Shopper positions are in tiles as decimals; the centre of tile (3, 5) is (3.5, 5.5). Directions are 0 east, 1 south, 2 west, 3 north.

## Sessions (`src/net/session.js`)
The host's browser owns the real state. Clients send commands. The host sends thirty small "advance" messages a second (one per step at normal speed): its step count, the commands it applied (each with the step it took effect at and who gave it), and a checksum. Clients replay them on their own copy, aiming to run a few steps behind the host (three, about a tenth of a second, on a steady link; more on one that stalls: the adaptive buffer below), and ask for a fresh copy if the checksum differs. A full copy is sent only when a player joins or leaves, after `fastForward`, on a mismatch, when a client falls more than 3 seconds behind, and once a minute as a safety net (every 10 seconds until 0.4). Messages over 48 KB (15 KB over the internet, the limit of the PeerJS library) are split and rejoined in `transports.js`. 'say', 'review' and 'report' reach a client when his replay reaches that moment. Game speed: `state.speed` is one of `SPEEDS` in `constants.js` (1, 2, 3 and, since 0.2.14, 5; no 4), set by any player with `{ c: 'speed', n }`; the host runs that many steps in the time of one, and the per-frame step limits (`HOST_MAX_STEPS`, the client's `MAX_STEPS`) grow with the top speed. While the game is paused (`state.pause`) both sides go on counting and sending steps as usual, so messages, copies, joins and chat all keep working, but each step changes nothing; the checksum includes the pause (only while there is one, so an unpaused game sums as before), and the host does not ask the director for its once-a-second `idle()` lines while paused (it asks when `state.tick` is a whole second, and the tick stands still). `fastForward` on a paused game runs its steps and returns. Never change `session.state` on the host directly while players are connected: go through `send()`. The user interface talks to a session and never to the simulation's state-changing functions directly:

| Member | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `session.state` | current state; `null` on a client until it has joined |
| `session.me` | this player's id (index into `state.players`) |
| `session.isHost` | true for `HostSession` |
| `session.send(cmd)` | issue a command |
| `session.sendCursor(x, y)` | tell others where you point, in tiles (decimals); throttle to about 10 a second |
| `session.on(type, fn)` | listen for a message; returns an unsubscribe function |
| `session.drainFx()` | simulation events since the last call, for effects. **Only the renderer calls this.** |
| `session.alpha()` | 0..1 progress between ticks; draw shoppers at `x + vx * alpha` |
| `session.start()` / `stop()` | run on a timer |
| `session.fastForward(n)` | host only: run n ticks at once (screenshots, tests) |
| `session.watchVisibility(source)` | host only: follow whether the page is hidden (`source` is the page's `document`, or anything with `hidden` and `addEventListener('visibilitychange', fn)`); returns a function that stops it. Also the constructor's `visibility` option. See "The host's page hidden" below |
| `session.away` / `setAway(on)` | host only: whether this host has said its page is hidden; `setAway` says it by hand (tests) |
| `session.hostAway` | client only: whether the host has said its page is hidden |
| `session.buffer`, `session.stats` | client only: the milliseconds behind the host it aims for now; counters (`checks`, `mismatches`, `copies`, `jumps`, `stops`, `want`) |
| `session.backToLobby({seed, director, holiday, cards})` | host only, once the game is won or lost: replace it with a fresh game in the lobby (same store and settings, the players still connected, in their old order) and send it to every client over the open connections. Returns false while the game is still on |

Messages delivered through `session.on`: `ready`, `lobby`, `say`, `review`, `report`, `players`, `nope`, `cursor`, `closed`, `away`. Their fields are listed at the top of `src/net/session.js`.

Keeping a joined player's picture moving (0.4, from the stutter investigation: `.claude/reports/2026-11-26-stutter-investigation.md`). The link is reliable and ordered, so one late or lost packet holds up everything sent after it; whenever the client has nothing left to play while a step is due, its picture stops.
- **Adaptive buffer** (`BUFFER` in `session.js`, exported for tests). The client aims to run `_want` steps (at 1x; times the speed) behind the host. It starts at `BUFFER.min` (3). When the stream runs dry for longer than `BUFFER.dryMs` (1.5 steps' time: a stop a player can see), that counts as a stop (`stats.stops`) and the aim moves `BUFFER.grow` (2) steps further back, up to `BUFFER.max` (9, 300 ms); after `BUFFER.calmSecs` (45 s) of play without a stop it comes one step closer, down to `min`. The aim never moves the picture: the client's existing rate (at most 15% slower, or faster, while the gap is off its aim) absorbs it. A paused game, and a host that has said its page is hidden, never count as stops. With the link model in `tools/perf/netbench.mjs` (300 s from event 16): a phone on a typical mobile link (50 ms, 40 ms jitter, 1% loss) went from 15 picture stops a minute (about 200 ms each) to 1.8 (about 100 ms), at about 130 ms more delay (300 ms behind instead of 167); a poor signal from 45 to 1.2; home broadband is unchanged (no stops, 133 ms behind).
- **Routine copy once a minute** (`FULL_EVERY_SECS`, 60; it was 10). In whole two-player games the checksum never disagreed, and a routine copy (40-55 KB in a busy event, 4-5 parts at the 15 KB limit) holds up the stream behind it on a slow uplink. Every other reason for a copy is unchanged (join, leave, `fastForward`, a mismatch, a client more than 3 s behind). With a 1 Mbps uplink: picture stops 5.8 a minute before, 0.6 after; traffic 6.2 KB/s before, 3.2 after.
- **The host's page hidden.** A browser runs a hidden page's timers about once a second, so while the host's page is hidden everybody else's game moves in one-second jerks (in the link model the picture stood still 48% of the time). The host session does not look at the page itself: it is handed something that knows (`new HostSession({ visibility: document })`, or `watchVisibility(document)`), and from then on sends `away {on}` to every client when the page is hidden (`on: true`) or back (`false`), and to a player who joins while it is hidden right after his welcome. Both sides emit `away {on}`; the client keeps it in `hostAway`. Showing it is the interface's job, and `main.js` still has to pass `document` (interface package).
- **What a second data channel for full copies would take** (not built): with PeerJS each `DataConnection` is its own peer connection, so a second one means a second `peer.connect` (its own handshake) per player in `transports.js`, and since the two would no longer keep their messages in one order, every advance message would need a sequence number so a client can tell which advance messages a copy already covers (today it relies on the order: a copy follows the advance message for its own step). Worth it only if copies become frequent again.

Going round again: in a multiplayer game the end screen's button reads "Back to the lobby" for the host (clients see that the host decides, and a "Quit to the menu" button). The host's `backToLobby()` sends each client a `lobby {me, n, s}` message: the new state, his id in it, and the step it starts at. The host moves its step count on by one first, so everything about the old game carries a smaller count; a client drops any `adv`, `snap` or `at` older than that step, and throws away whatever of the old game was still queued. Both sides then emit `lobby`, and the game screen takes down the end screen and shows the lobby as it does when the phase is `lobby`. The room code and link stay the same because no connection is closed. Clients cannot ask for it; there is no such command.

Creating sessions:
```js
import { HostSession, ClientSession } from './net/session.js';
import { createDirector } from './content/director.js';
import { hostOnline, joinOnline, hostLocal, joinLocal, makeRoomCode } from './net/transports.js';

// Solo, or the host of a multiplayer game:
const session = new HostSession({ name, director: createDirector() });
const room = await hostOnline(code, (conn) => session.accept(conn));   // omit for solo

// A friend joining:
const conn = await joinOnline(code);          // rejects with a readable Error
const session = new ClientSession(conn, name); // wait for the 'ready' message before drawing
```
`hostLocal` / `joinLocal` do the same between tabs of one browser, for testing without the internet. `hostOnline` / `joinOnline` need `vendor/peerjs.min.js` loaded by a plain `<script>` tag before the modules.

## Renderer (`src/render/renderer.js`)
```js
const renderer = createRenderer(canvas, session);
renderer.resize();                 // after the canvas element changes size
renderer.tileAt(clientX, clientY); // -> {x, y} tile under a mouse position, or null
renderer.draw(view);               // once per animation frame

// The camera belongs to the renderer. Maps can be much bigger than the screen.
renderer.cam;                      // read-only { x, y, zoom }: view centre in tiles, CSS pixels per tile
renderer.pan(dxTiles, dyTiles);
renderer.centerOn(xTiles, yTiles);
renderer.zoomBy(factor, clientX, clientY); // the tile under that point stays put
renderer.fit();                    // show the whole map
renderer.viewRect();               // -> { x0, y0, x1, y1 } tiles on screen
```
The camera never zooms out past the whole map or in past 72 pixels a tile, and never leaves the map by more than a tile. It starts on the whole map if tiles would be at least 24 pixels, otherwise at 30 pixels a tile. `src/ui/input.js` drives it (WASD, wheel, middle-drag, F, H). While the game is paused the renderer holds the fraction between ticks (`session.alpha()`) at its last value, so `F.time` stands still and shoppers do not jog on the spot.

Pause on screen (0.3.02): the pause button sits in the top bar just before the speed buttons (`.hud-clock` in `hud.js`: two bars, a play triangle on yellow while paused; under ☰ on a phone, beside the speeds), and `P` (`togglePause` in `input.js`, not while typing) does the same. The top bar's `fit()` may narrow it at step 4 but never drops it. While paused, `game.js` dims the floor (`.pause-veil`, which lets clicks through, so a click with a building held gets DEB's `paused` refusal) and shows the PAUSED banner over it, naming who paused; clicking the banner carries on. The root gets `.is-paused`, which greys out the build bar's buttons. The help screen and the README list the key.

`createMinimap(canvas, session, renderer)` in `src/render/minimap.js` returns `{ draw(), tileAt(clientX, clientY) }`: a small picture of the whole store with the visible part outlined. The game screen shows it when the store does not fit on screen.
`view` is supplied by the UI each frame:
```js
{
```

## Content (`src/content`)
- `lines.js` holds every line of dialogue. Add jokes there, not in code. A plain string is a common line; `u('...')` makes it uncommon (about a third as likely) and `r('...')` rare (about a tenth). The rules for adding lines are at the top of the file.
- `director.js` runs on the host, turns simulation events into `say`, `review` and `report` messages, and decides how often DEB speaks. Its output goes to every player, so everyone hears the same line with the same name in it. Each kind of remark is a topic in `TOPICS`: some are always said; the rest get less likely, and further apart, every time DEB has commented on that topic in this game. A line that has been used has its weight divided by `TALK.repeatPenalty` for the rest of the game. All the numbers are in `TALK`.
- Holidays (0.3): `HOLIDAY_LINES` in `lines.js` has, for each key of `HOLIDAYS`, four banks: `in` (the boss walks in), `out` (he storms off), `through` (he gets his refund) and `pause` (a joke in a build pause). In a game with `state.holiday`, the first walk-in, exit and refund of the game get a line from that holiday's bank instead of the usual one, and after that `TALK.holidayBoss` (60%) of them do; those lines name him with "the {k}" like the usual ones. Once each build pause, `TALK.holidayDelay` seconds after it starts and only while more than `TALK.holidayLast` seconds are left (clear of the friends' jabs and the ten-second warning), DEB may tell a `pause` joke: the `holiday` topic, a joke topic, so it fades over the game like any other (about four in a 20-event game, half of them in the first five pauses). Not in the training shift, and nothing at all with no holiday.
- The three friends: `FRIENDS` in `lines.js` has lines written for players named Bobb, Phantasm and Tusker (matched by exact name, ignoring capitals). They are used only when a player with that name is connected: a third of the times a line would name him, and now and then unprompted between waves.
- `nopeLine(why, name)` gives the line shown to one player when his command is refused.
- Pausing (0.3.02): `LINES.paused` (8 lines) and `LINES.resumed` (4), each naming the player who did it, are the note topics `paused` and `resumed` in `TOPICS`: one line per `pause` event at most, the first always, then fading like any note; their gap is counted in ticks of play, which stand still while paused, so pressing P over and over gets no more lines. `NOPE.paused` answers a command refused while paused.

## Sound (`src/audio`)
`createSound()` in `src/audio/sound.js` makes every sound with the Web Audio API and speaks DEB's lines with the browser's speech synthesis. `src/main.js` loads it with a dynamic import, so the game still runs (silently) if it fails. It listens to the session's `fx`, `say`, `review`, `report` and `players` messages; the interface calls `sound.play(name)` for its own sounds (`select`, `place`, `remove`, `rotate`, `rebuild`, `chat`, `nope`, `click`, `tutorial`). Frequent sounds vary on every play (three versions each, with pitch, length and timbre nudged by a random seed), and each kind of shopper has its own voice in `src/audio/shoppers.js`. `src/audio/crowd.js` holds the rules for many sounds at once: spacing, thinning, lowering the level as they pile up, merging a mass walkout or a run of sales into one sound, and a cap on how much is played. It contains no audio code, so the test page runs the same rules. Every effect is one entry in the table in `src/audio/effects.js` (the sounds added in 0.2 are built in `src/audio/newfx.js`, the boss's and endless mode's in `src/audio/boss.js`, the Midnight Release's and a ripped pack's in `src/audio/trader.js`, the deal of the cards' in `src/audio/cards.js` (played by the interface with `sound.play(name, opts)`); `bossOut` carries the sound when a boss is driven out, so `sound.js` drops the `left` that arrives with it). The `pause` event plays `pause` (a record scratch and a turntable winding down) or `unpause` (spinning back up) for every player; the store music, the only sound that loops, sinks to `PAUSED` (0.3) of its level while `state.pause` is set (`pauseDuck` in `sound.js`, also asked whenever the music is scheduled, so a game joined or resumed while paused is quiet too). Nobody can check sound by ear in an automated run, so `tools/sound-test.html` renders each effect offline and measures it (silent, clipping, clicks, too long); keep it at ALL PASS and add new sounds to the table.

Sound levels (0.3.03). Each player sets his own sound in the menu behind the speaker button (`createSoundMenu` in `src/ui/hud.js`): the overall Volume (the master) and a level from 0 to 1 for each of six groups, Money, Shoppers, Security and machines, Announcements, Store music and DEB's voice (`GROUPS` in `src/audio/groups.js`, which also reads and keeps the settings and has no audio code, so Node tests run it). Every effect belongs to one group by `GROUP_OF`, beside the table in `effects.js` (a test fails if one has none; DEB's blips are in `voice`, with her speech). `createChain` in `mix.js` gives each group a bus feeding the effects bus, and the music a level after the gain that ducks it, so a moved slider changes a sound already playing (50 ms glide) and the ducks multiply with the music's level. `sound.js` does not build a sound at all for a group at 0 (nor anything at master 0), and it never reaches the crowd gate, so the other groups are thinned as before; the music stops being scheduled at 0; DEB speaks at master times her level. `sound.get(key)` / `sound.set(key, value)` take `volume` and the six group keys as numbers (clamped); `sound.on(group)` says whether a group can be heard; `sound.toggle(key)` is the speaker mark beside a row (0, or back to its last level above 0, which is saved too); `sound.preview(group)` plays one short sample of a group at its level when a slider is let go (one at a time; DEB says a line of `SOUND_CHECK` in `lines.js`). The settings stay in the player's browser (localStorage `returnsDept.sound`), never in the game state. Saves from before 0.3.03 held three switches: `sfx: false` becomes the four effect groups at 0, `music: false` and `voice: false` their level at 0; only levels are written back. With the defaults (everything at 1, master 0.7) every sound comes out exactly as before; `tools/sound-test.html` checks that, and that each group at 0 is silent and at 0.5 half as loud.

## Training shift (`src/tutorial`, `src/ui/tutorial.js`)
A game created with `createGame({ training: true })` has no build timer, cannot be lost, and only runs the waves queued with `queueTrainingWave()`. `src/tutorial/lessons.js` holds the lessons as data plus a small engine (fourteen lessons since 0.4, whose removal step 3 took out the six about the old factory. Part 1: a wall of Crate Stacks (the maze), the route that can never be blocked, and every security building against Coupon Clippers, the Sprinkler where a crate is removed (the Remove tool), super ones in "Super shoppers" (a third Sprinkler; one sentence says supplies from The Back make security stronger), and the Barcode Laser down a column where Extreme Couponers queue. Part 2: Dads (five, then three Dads on a Mission; since stage 4a they walk straight through the maze and most reach a till), the Forklift Turret, which gets one of two Dads on a Mission (both get through without it; the lesson says Razor Wire from The Back is what stops dads for good), and the Gym Bro with the Rebuild tool. "Training complete" ends with one sentence about The Back (Tab); lessons that teach it are a later stage. Only those three kinds of shopper, and their supers, are sent in, and a test holds the shift to that and to every building in BUILD being placed by the player; a lesson can count what shoppers did, such as dads slipping or things wrecked, and can run commands after its wave); each lesson has a `solve()` list of commands, which the tests use to play the whole tutorial and check that every lesson can be completed and shows what its text claims. If you change a building's rules or numbers, run the tests: a lesson that no longer works will fail there. Plain-language explanations of buildings and shoppers live in `src/content/help.js`.

## The Back (0.4, built in stages; `PLAN.md` says which stages are done)
From 0.4 the factory is a second floor under the shop, **The Back**. It makes no money. It turns returned junk into supplies and delivers them to the security buildings above, which are stronger while stocked. This section is the contract for it. The old shop-floor factory it replaced is gone (0.4, removal step 3); the earlier sections describe the game as it is.

### Two floors, one grid
- `state.back` is a flat array like `state.grid`, the same size and indexing: a Back part or 0 per tile. `state.backVer` goes up whenever a part is added, removed or turned, and derived data is cached against it (as path maps are against `gridVer`).
- A tile of The Back can hold a part unless `state.tiles[i] === '#'`: the store's walls and shelves go all the way down. Tills and entrances have open floor under them. Nothing in The Back blocks shoppers and no route check is needed to place a part.
- Back parts are kinds in `BUILD` with `back: true` and group `'back'`. They are bought, owned (`o`), removed and refunded like any building. `SHOP_KINDS` lists every other kind; the build bar, hotkeys, handbook and tests that list the shop's buildings read it. Of the cards, shrinkflation and staffdiscount change a Back part's price, and since 0.4's removal step 2 sweatshop and rentacop do too (their `'back'` group); a price a card changed is kept as `c`, as on the shop floor.
- The `place`, `remove` and `rotate` commands take `f: 1` to mean The Back (no `f`, or 0, means the shop floor; any other `f` is refused with `'bad'`). `canPlaceBack(state, k, x, y, pid, d)` is `canPlace`'s partner. A shop kind in The Back, or a Back kind on the shop floor, is refused with `'kind'`, and so is placing a Returns Drop or a Dumbwaiter (`fixed: true` in `BUILD`); removing one is refused with `'fixed'`. A belt laid on a belt turns it, free. Events: `placed` and `removed` with `f: 1`.
- Which floor a player is looking at is not in the state. It is local to his screen (`view.floor`, 0 shop, 1 The Back), switched with Tab. The floor he is not on is drawn faintly (about 25%).
- The engine lives in `src/sim/back.js`. `stepBack(state, ev)` runs once per tick, in the build pause and during events alike, before the security buildings fire (opening the doors early skips nothing ahead: removal step 2). Its event is `binned {i, v, by}`: a Clearance Bin on tile i was paid v for a supply. Besides the functions named below it exports `JUNK`, `dropTicks`, `makeTicks`, `rebrandTicks`, `stockCap`, `newPart`, `overpassDirs(state, i)` and `beltShape(state, i)` (for drawing), and for stock `SUPPLY_TRACKS` (track by supply type), `STOCK_TRACKS`, `usesPer`, `unitsIn`, `usesIn`, `takesSupply`, `addStock`, `useStock`, `useWork`, `stealStock`, `syncLifts`, `syncAllLifts`; `makeDrops` is in `maps.js`.

### Items
- An item is a small whole number. `1` is returned junk. A supply is `10 * type + tier`, type 1 Bottled Spite (track `dmg`), 2 Rage Cola (`spd`), 3 Extension Cord (`rng`), 4 Duct Tape (`dur`), 5 Razor Wire (`wire`), tier 1 Store Brand, 2 Name Brand, 3 Premium, 4 Platinum. So Premium Rage Cola is 23. `back.js` exports `itemCode`, `itemType`, `itemTier`.
- Nothing stores items except belts in motion, the one-item hands and slots named below, and the dumbwaiters. There is no warehouse.

### Belts (`conv`, shown as "Belt")
- A belt tile is `{k:'conv', o, d, it:[code, pos, code, pos, ...]}`: the items on it, front one first. `pos` is a whole number from 0 (entering) to `BACK.sub` (leaving), so there are no fractions to drift between browsers.
- Numbers, all in `BACK` in `constants.js`: `sub` 240 units per tile; `gap` 80, the least distance between two items, so a tile holds three; `step` 16 units a tick, which is 2 tiles a second; `end` = `sub - gap / 2`, where an item waits when it cannot go on. The same table has `internSecs`, `dropSecs`, `dropMul` (a guess, to tune) and `binPay`.
- Movement must not depend on tile order. Lanes are stepped **downstream first**: a lane is stepped after the lane it hands into, so a full line moves as one and no item moves twice in a tick. The order is derived from the layout and cached against `backVer` (never stored in the state). A ring of belts is broken at its lowest tile index: what is handed into that tile (a lane or a Splitter) before its turn stays put that tick. So that a ring with no free space still circulates at full speed, the one hand-over across the break assumes the last item of the lane it joins moves a `step` on that tick; if the ring is held up and it does not, those two items sit up to one `step` closer than `gap` until the ring moves again (never closer, and never more than that one pair).
- Turning a belt (or anything next to it) can leave two items briefly closer than `gap` across a join, until the line moves on. An Overpass lane's direction is read from the layout again whenever a neighbour changes, so after an edit the items on it may run the other way.
- **Corners.** A belt with nothing feeding it from behind and exactly one feeder at one side is a corner (`beltShape`: 1 fed from its right, -1 from its left, 0 straight). A feeder is any part whose output goes into the tile: a belt facing it, an Overpass lane running into it, a Splitter's way, a Returns Drop, a machine's or a Rebrander's front; not an Unpaid Intern. Worked out from the layout and cached against `backVer`. Items from a corner's feeding side come on exactly as from behind.
- A front item heading into a belt as if from behind (straight on, or round a corner) may advance to `sub + (that belt's last pos - gap)`, which keeps the gap across the join. Once past `sub` it is handed over with the overshoot as its new `pos`, so it travels the same distance a tile on a corner as on a straight. Heading into anything else it advances to `end` and is offered each tick.
- A belt takes items from behind and from both sides, never head-on. A merge (a feeder from behind and one at a side, or one at each side) takes side items at `sub / 2` if no item is within `gap` of that spot. A belt fed from behind and from the side gives way to neither: whichever lane is stepped first that tick wins, and the order is fixed.
- The step is written as a function over one lane array, so a second lane (`it2`, the two-sided belts of Factorio) can be added later without a rewrite.

### Handing over
- Everything that passes an item on uses two functions in `back.js`: `wants(state, i, code, dir, over)` (would tile `i` take this item, arriving travelling in direction `dir`, right now?) and `give(state, i, code, dir, over)`, which does it. `over` (default 0) is how far past the tile's edge the item already is: a belt handing on as if from behind passes its overshoot; everything else 0. `wants` has no side effects. All hand-overs go through them, so a new part only has to answer those two.
- **Splitter** `{k:'split', o, d, h, r}`: takes one item from behind into `h` (0 when empty), and each tick offers it to the tiles in front, to its left and to its right, starting after the one that took the last item (`r`), skipping any that do not want it. One belt in, up to three out, shared evenly among the ways that are open.
- **Overpass** `{k:'over', o, a:[...], b:[...]}`: two belt lanes that do not mix, `a` running east-west and `b` north-south. Which way each runs is read from the layout (the neighbour that feeds into it; east and south win a tie), not stored.
- **Unpaid Intern** `{k:'intern', o, d, h, t}`: stands between two tiles. With an empty hand he takes from the belt behind him the front-most item that the tile in front of him `wants`, holds it for `BACK.internSecs`, then gives it (onto a belt: at 0 from behind, otherwise at the middle). He takes from belts only, not Overpass lanes.
- **Returns Drop** `{k:'drop', d, t, lv}`: fixed by the map, owned by nobody, cannot be removed, can be turned by anyone. Only a game made with a map has them (the bare default map, used by training and most tests, has none for now). `makeDrops` in `maps.js` puts one under each Loading Bay patch of the old marked floor (`factoryPatches`, see "Where the old marked floor was"), working the patches out itself (tiles up to two apart are one patch, so a patch a shelf cuts in two gets one drop), on the patch tile nearest its middle, facing the store's middle from top to bottom, or the next way that is not a wall. Every `dropTicks(lv)` (`BACK.dropSecs`, times `BACK.dropMul` for each cash level `lv`) it puts one junk on the tile in front if that tile wants it, and waits while it does not.
- **`dropUp {x, y}`** (a command, any store player, not Head Office): pays `dropUpCost(lv)` = `round(BACK.dropUpCost * BACK.dropUpGrow ** lv)` (150, 240, 384, 614, 983) to raise that drop's `lv` by one, up to `BACK.dropUpMax` (5). A wait under way is cut to the new time. Counted in the payer's `spent`; nothing sells it back. Refused with `'over'`, `'bounds'`, `'empty'` (no drop there), `'max'`, `'cash'`. Event `dropUp {x, y, lv, by, paid}`.
- **Clearance Bin** `{k:'bin', o}`: takes anything from any side. Junk pays nothing; a supply pays `BACK.binPay[tier]` (times `cardBin`: the lootbox card), shared like any income (`earn`), counted in `ws.earned` and its owner's `earned`.
- **Machines** (Spite Still `still`, Cola Cannery `cannery`, Cord Spooler `spooler`, Tape Winder `winder`, Barb Bender `bender`; $40 each; `BUILD[k].supply` is the type): `{k, o, d, n, t, out}`. Each takes junk (nothing else) from any side but its front while fewer than 2 wait (`n`), works one at a time (`t` ticks left), and every `BACK.makeSecs` (2; `makeTicks(state)`, which the artisanal card slows) turns one into its supply at tier 1. Finishing, handing over and starting the next happen in the same tick. The product goes on the tile in front if that tile wants it; otherwise it waits in `out` and nothing new starts.
- **Rebrander** `{k:'rebrand', o, d, q:[codes], t, out}` ($50): takes any supply below tier 4 (never junk) from any side but its front, while fewer than 2 wait. `q` holds them, the one being worked first while `t > 0` (so up to 3 in all). It puts out the same supply one tier higher after `BACK.rebrandSecs[tier going in]` (4, 8, 16 s; `rebrandTicks(state, tier)`: asseenontv faster, artisanal slower), with `out` as a machine's.
- Machines and Rebranders are stepped before whatever feeds them (downstream first, like belts). An Unpaid Intern or a belt pointing straight into one feeds it, and the same goes for a Dumbwaiter.

### Dumbwaiters and stock
- Every tile of a security building whose kind has `lift: true` (Pepper Sprinkler, Forklift Turret, Barcode Laser, Wet Floor Sign, Free Samples, TV Salesman) has a **Dumbwaiter** under it: `{k:'lift', a}` in `state.back`, `a` being the building's anchor tile. Crate Stacks and the old factory have none. Nobody buys, removes or turns one, and no part can go on its tile (`'occupied'`).
- `syncLifts(state, tiles)` makes the Dumbwaiters on those tiles match the shop floor: one under every tile of a standing `lift` building, and of the rubble of one; none anywhere else. `commands.js` calls it whenever a building is put down or taken away and whenever a rubble record goes; `wreck` calls it for a record that falls off the end of the 400-long list. It never puts a Dumbwaiter on another part. `backVer` goes up when anything changes.
- A `lift` building cannot be placed where The Back holds any part but a Dumbwaiter (`canPlace` and the rebuild's check answer `'back'`). Dumbwaiters already there can only belong to rubble, which the new building clears.
- Rubble: a wrecked building's Dumbwaiters stay, wanting nothing, so nothing can be built under it and a rebuild is never blocked by The Back. They go when the rubble record goes without being rebuilt (built over by anything, tidied away, off the end of the list); a rebuild takes them over. Stock is lost when a building is wrecked or removed (the rubble record does not carry it).
- A Dumbwaiter wants a supply (`takesSupply(b, code)`) if its building is standing, has that track (`BUILD[k].up`; every `lift` kind also takes `wire`), and holds fewer than `stockCap(state)` units of it (`BACK.stockUnits`, 6; the regifting card adds 2). Units are counted per run, a unit partly used counting as a whole one (`unitsIn`), so a building takes a new unit only once a whole one is used. It takes from any side.
- Stock is on the building: `b.s = { dmg: [tier, uses, tier, uses, ...], ... }`, runs in the order they arrived, whole numbers. A unit arriving adds `usesPer(kind, track, tier)` uses to the last run if the tier matches, else starts a run. `usesPer` is `BUILD[k].shots` for `dmg`, `spd` and `rng` (sprinkler 20, forklift 6, laser 2, sign 10, samples 8, salesman 8), `BACK.tapeHits[tier]` (1 to 4) for `dur`, `BACK.wireTurns[tier]` (3, 6, 10, 15) for `wire`; with the artisanal card each times 1.25, rounded up. A track used up is deleted, and `s` with it once empty.
- Levels: `levelIn(b, t)` in `upgrades.js` is the level the stock gives (`stockLevel`: `BACK.tierLevel[tier of the first run]`, `[0, 2, 4, 6, 8]`); bought upgrades are gone (removal step 1). So `statsOf` and its formulas are unchanged, and an unstocked building is exactly the 0.3 building with nothing bought. Duct Tape does not raise `dur`'s level: its uses soak hits instead (below). Stock is never money: `spentOn`, the sell-back and `rebuildDeal` read only the price paid.
- Use: each shot a tower fires (however many it hits), each shopper Free Samples stop, each pitch of the TV Salesman that stops a shopper or hurries him past, and each shopper a Wet Floor Sign slows (once, as he steps on) or trips takes one use from each of `dmg`, `spd` and `rng` that is stocked (`useWork`). Each gym bro hit on a building with `dur` stock takes one use instead of counting as a hit (`b.hit` does not rise; the `smash` event has `tape: 1`, and its `left` counts the tape still on). Razor Wire is used by dads (below).
- When a track runs out completely, `stockOut {i, t}` (anchor tile, track) goes on the tick's events.
- Shoplifters and Card Traders steal stock from the buildings they pass, a unit at a time (removal step 2: see "Shoplifting" above).

### Razor Wire and dads
(Simulation built in stage 4a, 0.4.00: `src/sim/wire.js`, the dad maps in `path.js`, the commands in `commands.js`, the use in `step.js`; tests in `test/wire.test.js`. The interface, stage 4b: drawing in `src/render/wire.js` and `wireplan.js`, the tools and the sheet's picker in `src/ui/wiretool.js`, `input.js` and `touch.js`; see "Renderer"; tests in `test/wire-ui.test.js`.)
- Dads walk through every building; only the store's walls and live wire stop them. Nobody else is affected by wire in any way, and nothing else about dads changed: single file, no samples, no signs (they may slip), gawking at an Exhibitionist, the TV Salesman's pitch, the eyesup card (by the `DAD` map). A dad walking over to a TV Salesman's stand (`walkToStand`) steps only on tiles both `DAD` and `NORMAL` allow, so he watches from beside the stand, not inside it.
- A building whose kind has a Dumbwaiter (`lift: true`; `carriesWire(k)`) may have a wire direction `b.wd` (0 to 3, the numbering of `DIRS`: a direction across the store, not relative to the building's facing), absent when none. Crate Stacks cannot carry wire, but can be under another building's. Its wire covers the building's own tiles plus a strip `BACK.wireReach` (3) tiles long beyond its edge that way, as wide as the side of the building facing that way: a line out from every tile on that side, each stopping before the first wall tile (`'#'`) and at the edge of the map (tills and entrances are floor; covering an entrance is refused, below). `wireCovers(state, tiles, wd)` works it out from any footprint, `wireTiles(state, x, y, d)` for the building on any tile of it in any direction (for a preview before the command is sent; `d` left out is its own), `buildingTiles(state, ai)` is a standing building's footprint.
- The wire is **live** while the building holds any `wire` stock (`wireLive(b)`: it has `wd` and `b.s.wire`), **planned** otherwise. Live wire stops dads on every tile it covers, open floor included.
- Where the wire is: `wireMap(state)` gives `live` (per tile, the anchor of the building whose live wire covers it, the lowest anchor index if several, or -1) and `plan` (per tile, how many buildings' wire covers it, live or planned), worked out from the grid and cached against `gridVer` in a WeakMap, never stored in the state. So `gridVer` goes up whenever wire changes: the `wire` command, a building put down, removed, wrecked or turned (as before), and a wire going live or dead: `addStock` when Razor Wire arrives at an empty track of a building with `wd` (a Dumbwaiter's `give`, any card's free stock), `stealStock` when a thief takes the last unit, `useWire` when the last use is spent. A dad re-routes on the next tick (the path maps for a tick are taken at its start).
- Two dad maps: `DAD` (walls and live wire block) and `DADPLAN` (walls and every wire, live or planned). The `wire` command, `{ c: 'wire', x, y, d }` (`d` 0 to 3 sets the direction, -1 clears it; any tile of the building), is allowed at any time in a running game (the build phase or a sales event, not paused), to any store player (as `rotate` is: Head Office cannot), and free. It is refused with `'blocked'` if, on `DADPLAN` with the change made, any entrance, open or shut, has no way to the tills or a bank of tills is cut off from the others (`dadPlanCheck(state, plan)` in `path.js`, with `planWith(state, was, now)` in `wire.js`; the same guarantees `canPlace` gives ordinary shoppers on `NORMAL`). Live wire is always a part of planned wire, so passing this means the live map can never be cut off. Clearing a wire, or setting the direction it already has, never is (a harmless yes with no event). `canWire(state, pid, x, y, d)` gives the same reasons: `'over'`, `'lobby'`, `'cards'`, `'paused'`, `'bad'` (no such player, or `d` not a whole number from -1 to 3), `'office'`, `'bounds'`, `'empty'` (no building there), `'kind'` (a building that cannot carry wire), `'blocked'`. Event `wire {x, y, d, by, live}` (the anchor tile; `live` 1 if it already holds Razor Wire).
- Buildings coming and going: a new building has no wire, so placing one never blocks dads. A building removed or wrecked loses its wire; the rubble remembers `wd`, and a rebuild (the Rebuild order or placing the same kind on the rubble) brings the wire back only if `dadPlanCheck` allows it then, and otherwise rebuilds without it (`restoreWire` in `commands.js`). Turning a building re-checks its wire if what it covers would change, and refuses the turn with `'blocked'` if the plan would cut a way; since a wire's direction is the store's and the Barcode Laser turns end for end on its own footprint, no turn changes it today (the check is a guard for any building that may one day turn onto other tiles).
- Where dads walk (the use rule; `dadDecide` in `path.js`, `dadMove` in `step.js`): **dads are dumb, they walk into the wire.** A dad does not take the wire-aware way from the door: he heads the way he would with no wire, bumps into live wire, is turned away there (that is when the wire is charged), and only then goes round. At his tile t, with `ghost(t)` his distance on the walls-only map and `dad(t)` on `DAD`, he is *obstructed* when `dad(t) > ghost(t)`. Each time he picks his next tile: (0) if t has no way to a till on `DAD` (he is under wire, or shut in a pocket of floor by it), he steps by the walls-only map and nothing is charged or changed (he climbs out the way he was going); (1) if he is not obstructed, he steps by `DAD` and is no longer going round; (2) obstructed and not yet going round: if his next walls-only step g is not under live wire he steps to g (toward where his straight way is blocked); if it is, he *bumps*: the building whose live wire covers g (the lowest anchor) is charged one use, unless it has charged him already, `turned` fires at g, he is going round from now on (`s.dm` 1), and he takes a `DAD` step instead; (3) obstructed and going round: if g is under live wire whose building has not charged him yet, it is charged now (he tests each stretch of fence he walks along), then he steps by `DAD`. Both steps use his usual tie-break (`nextOn`, the neighbours in `DIRS` order from `id & 3`). Each step makes progress: walking toward the wire the walls-only distance falls, going round and walking free his `DAD` distance falls, and on a layout that holds still a free dad never becomes obstructed again. A till under live wire is bumped like any wire tile.
- Memory: `s.rw` is a list of the anchors that have charged him (absent until one has), so no wire charges the same dad twice, however its tiles and other wires' interleave. `state.wireGen` (absent reads as 0) goes up whenever wire is laid differently: a `wire` command that changes a direction, a wired building removed, wrecked or rebuilt with its wire (`wireChanged` in `wire.js`). A dad keeps the value he last saw in `s.wg` (absent while it is 0); when it differs he forgets `s.rw` and `s.dm`. So flipping wire about to stall dads costs wire every time, and a stale anchor from a removed building cannot skip a charge on a new one at the same tile.
- A charge is one use of the building's `wire` stock (`useWire`); `BACK.wireTurns[tier]` (3, 6, 10, 15) dads use up one unit (`usesPer`; the artisanal card adds a quarter). Spending the last use drops the wire to planned, moves `gridVer` on and sends the usual `stockOut {i, t: 'wire'}`; the dads behind are then no longer obstructed there. Event `turned {i, t, x, y, id}`: `i` the wire's building, `t` the wire tile (the bumped or tested one), `(x, y)` its middle, `id` the dad; for every charge, and for a bump on wire that had already charged him (then with `free: 1`).
- `dadRoute(state, from, planned = false)` in `path.js` traces the tiles a dad who walks in at tile `from` would walk to a till under this rule, with the fixed tie-break (the first choice in `DIRS` order: a dad whose id is a multiple of 4), the wire not used up on the way: with the live wire (`planned` false) or with every planned wire as if live (true). The distance maps alone no longer say where dads walk, so this is what the interface draws as the two dad routes. Cached against `gridVer` (every change to the wire moves it); the list returned is shared and must not be changed.
- Exports for the interface (`src/sim/index.js`): `wireTiles`, `wireCovers`, `wireLive`, `wireMap`, `carriesWire`, `canWire`, `getDadDist`, `getDadPlanDist`, `DADPLAN`, `dadRoute`, `dadDecide`; and for crates `canReinforce`, `reinforceCost`.
- Drawn (stage 4b, see "Renderer"): both dad routes from each open entrance with `dadRoute`, the live one solid and the planned one faint where it differs; the wire itself; the Wire and Reinforce tools and the sheet's picker.
- Sync: `s.rw` (now a list), `s.dm`, `s.wg` and `state.wireGen` are plain JSON and travel with the state. The checksum in `src/net/session.js` mixes `s.rw` as written for a single anchor; it should mix every entry of the list, and `s.dm`, `s.wg` and `wireGen` (left to whoever owns that file).

### Money
- Income (built in removal step 2): the event pay, 150 + 45 an event on Normal, to every store player in full; the speed bonus, up to half of that, its clock running from the last shopper in (`state.lastIn`) to a clear floor, all of it within 10 seconds, nothing at par (60 s, 120 in a boss event), scaled by the share of shoppers stopped rather than refunded; boss bounties; and the Clearance Bin. See "Event pay and the speed bonus" above. (The old shop-floor factory's goods, and selling them at a Shipping Chute, went in removal step 3.)
- Starting an event early no longer runs the factory ahead (`skipBuild` is gone), so the pause is refill time and starting early gives it up.

### Sync
- The Back is part of the state, so it is sent, saved and replayed like the rest. The checksum (`session.js`) covers it in one pass: `backVer`, the number of items in The Back (hands included), the sums of their positions and codes weighted by a number from their tile, the Splitters' `h` and `r`, the Interns' `h` and `t`, the drops' `t` and `lv`, the machines' `n`, `t` and `out`, the Rebranders' `q`, `t` and `out`, each Dumbwaiter's `a`, and every building's stock (each run's tier and uses, by `STOCK_TRACKS`; nothing for an unstocked building). Since stage 4a it also mixes in a building's wire direction `wd` and a crate's reinforcement `r` (with its `c`), and a dad's `rw`, each only where there is one, so a game without them sums as before. Outside The Back it also covers the speed bonus's clock (`lastIn`, while there is one) and the event's `stopped` and `escaped` counts.
- A command's values come from a player's browser: any used to look something up by name (a kind, a track, a design, a size, a difficulty, a player) is checked with `Object.hasOwn` or a list's `includes` (`buildDef(k)` for kinds), never a bare `obj[x]`.
- `stepBack` walks a cached list of parts, not the whole grid, and makes no garbage per tick: the largest store is 121 x 67 tiles and every client simulates at up to 5x speed.
